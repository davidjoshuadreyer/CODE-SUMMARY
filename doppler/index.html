Example method & data sources

This saved result uses direct spectrogram scoring, reported aircraft velocity, one shared recording clock, a constant source tone for each event, and externally estimated receiver height. Candidate methods were compared on held-out audio blocks. Sound speed is fixed at 343 m/s; wind and atmospheric refraction are not modelled. A position-and-velocity-consistent alternative gave roughly 110 m error, illustrating remaining model sensitivity.

Aircraft data: ADSB.lol daily archive (ODbL). Streets and coastline: © OpenStreetMap contributors; regional geometry: US Atlas / Census. Heights: USGS EPQS and NOAA GEOID18. No recording upload is needed to view this precomputed example.

WHAT THE MODEL DOES

From sound to a candidate place.

01

Measure

The full recording is scanned for moving tonal peaks. Stationary background tones are suppressed, supported traces are joined, and curved Doppler models are fitted automatically.

02

Predict

For each candidate receiver, flight positions become a three-dimensional trajectory. Sound is shifted according to radial velocity at emission, then placed at its arrival time.

03

Compare

Fit an emitted tone to each event, preserve their shared clock, and rank locations by frequency error. Compare the fitted curves, not just the winning coordinate.

Open-source algorithms worth exploring

librosa piptrack (ISC) refines spectral peak frequencies; its reassigned spectrogram can sharpen time/frequency localization. ssqueezepy ridge extraction follows continuous ridges using dynamic programming; inspect its component license notices before reusing the whole package. aubio (GPL) offers several pitch trackers.

This page uses an independent browser implementation: Hann-window FFT, background suppression, continuous peak tracking, sub-bin peak interpolation, and nonlinear Doppler-shape fitting. Manual refinement is optional. It does not bundle those Python/C libraries. Tracking frequency is the measurement step; the flight model tests its Doppler explanation.

Scope of this first version

Supported: local MOV/MP4 AAC audio extraction, incremental MP3 decoding, automatic full-recording candidate detection, observed and fitted curve inspection, guided ridge refinement, curve import/export, CSV/JSON/readsb tracks, automatic choice among imported aircraft, shared time-offset search, and evidence export. Assumes a stationary receiver, constant sound speed, constant source pitch within each event, and piecewise-linear flight segments.

The Python companion provides archive retrieval and bounded date-wide indexing. Not yet implemented: verified aircraft classification, terrain grids, or weather/refraction correction. Automatic detections are candidates, not verified aircraft identities. Synthetic tests and sample-recording checks do not establish a general detection or location accuracy rate.
