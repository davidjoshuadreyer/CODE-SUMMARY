WHAT THE MODEL DOES

From sound to a candidate place.

01

Measure

A spectrogram separates sound into frequency over time. Follow a propeller tone through its rise and fall, with manual guide points when needed.

02

Predict

For each candidate receiver, flight positions become a three-dimensional trajectory. Sound is shifted according to radial velocity at emission, then placed at its arrival time.

03

Compare

Fit an emitted tone to each event, preserve their shared clock, and rank locations by frequency error. Compare the fitted curves, not just the winning coordinate.

Open-source algorithms worth exploring

librosa piptrack (ISC) refines spectral peak frequencies; its reassigned spectrogram can sharpen time/frequency localization. ssqueezepy ridge extraction follows continuous ridges using dynamic programming; inspect its component license notices before reusing the whole package. aubio (GPL) offers several pitch trackers.

This page uses an independent browser implementation: Hann-window FFT, continuity-constrained ridge tracking, manual anchors, and sub-bin peak interpolation. It does not bundle those Python/C libraries. Tracking frequency is the measurement step; the flight model tests its Doppler explanation.

Scope of this first version

Supported: local media decoding, spectrogram inspection, guided ridge extraction, curve import/export, CSV/JSON/readsb tracks, automatic choice among imported aircraft, shared time-offset search, and evidence export. Assumes a stationary receiver, constant sound speed, constant source pitch within each event, and piecewise-linear flight segments.

Not yet implemented: archive-wide flight retrieval, date-wide indexing, automatic aircraft-event detection, terrain grids, silence filtering, or weather/refraction correction. Results have been checked on synthetic data; this is not a measured real-world accuracy claim.
