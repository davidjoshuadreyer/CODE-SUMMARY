S22 · P1pH Meter Bias
Question (Set 22 · P1) Suppose that an engineer is interested in testing the bias in a pH meter. Data are collected on a neutral substance (pH = 7.0). A sample of measurements is taken, with the data as follows:
7.00, 7.10, 6.97, 7.00, 7.03, 7.01, 7.01, 6.98, 7.08
Is there sufficient evidence to indicate that the pH meter is biased? Use α = 0.05.
🔍 Formula choice "Bias" = testing if the mean deviates from a known value → single mean t-test (σ unknown, n=9 < 30). "Bias" can go either way → two-tailed: H₀: μ=7.0, H₁: μ≠7.0. Sheet → S22: Case III (σ unknown, n<30 → T)
Step 1. n=9, x̄ = (7.00+7.10+6.97+7.00+7.03+7.01+7.01+6.98+7.08)/9 = 63.18/9 = 7.0200
Step 2. s = 0.0436 (compute from deviations or calculator)
Step 3. t = (7.0200 – 7.0) / (0.0436/√9) = 0.020 / 0.01453 = 1.38, df = 8
Step 4. t-table: t0.10,8=1.397, t0.10,8=1.397. Since |1.38| < 1.397 → p-value > 0.20
Do not reject H₀. No significant evidence of bias.

S22 · P3Standardized Test Completion Time
Question (Set 22 · P3) Past experience indicates that the time required for high school seniors to complete a standardized test is a normal random variable with a mean of 35 minutes. If a random sample of 20 high school seniors took an average of 33.1 minutes to complete this test with a standard deviation of 4.3 minutes, test the hypothesis at the 0.05 level of significance that μ = 35 minutes against the alternative that μ < 35 minutes.
🔍 Formula choice "μ < 35" → one-tailed lower test. n=20, σ unknown → t-test. df = n–1 = 19. Sheet → S22: Case III (σ unknown, n<30 → T)
Step 1. t = (33.1 – 35) / (4.3/√20) = –1.9 / 0.9613 = –1.98, df = 19
Step 2. One-tailed lower: t0.025,19=2.093, t0.05,19=1.729. Since 1.729 < |–1.98| < 2.093 → 0.025 < p-value < 0.05

SET 23 — Single Proportion (z-test) Use when: yes/no outcome, 1 sample → z = (p̂ – p₀)/√(p₀q₀/n)
S23 · P1Heat Pump Installation Claim
Question (Set 23 · P1) A builder claims that heat pumps are installed in 70% of all homes being constructed today in the city of Richmond, Virginia. Would you agree with this claim if a random survey of new homes in this city showed that 8 out of 15 had heat pumps installed? Use a 0.10 level of significance.
🔍 Formula choice "Proportion" (installed or not = yes/no) + testing a specific p₀ → single proportion z-test. "Agree with claim" = two-tailed (could be too high or too low). Sheet → Single Proportion card → z_obs row
Step 1. p̂ = 8/15 = 0.5333    p₀ = 0.70, q₀ = 0.30
Step 2. z = (0.5333 – 0.70) / √(0.70×0.30/15) = –0.1667 / 0.1183 = –1.41
Step 3. Two-tailed: p-value = 2×P(Z < –1.41) = 2×0.0793 p-value = 0.1586
p-value = 0.1586 > α = 0.10 → Do not reject H₀. No significant disagreement with the 70% claim.
S23 · P2Lasagna Preference
Question (Set 23 · P2) A marketing expert for a pasta-making company believes that 40% of pasta lovers prefer lasagna. If 9 out of 20 pasta lovers choose lasagna over other pastas, what can be concluded about the expert's claim? Use α = 0.05.

S24 · P1Two Populations, σ Known
Question (Set 24 · P1) A random sample of size n₁ = 25, taken from a normal population with a standard deviation σ₁ = 5.2, has a mean x̄₁ = 81. A second random sample of size n₂ = 36, taken from a different normal population with a standard deviation σ₂ = 3.4, has a mean x̄₂ = 76. Test the hypothesis at the 0.06 level of significance that μ₁ = μ₂ against the alternative that μ₁ ≠ μ₂.
🔍 Formula choice Two separate groups + σ (population s.d.) given → use z-test (not t). Two-tailed since "≠". Sheet → S24/25: Case I (σ known → Z)
Step 1. ese = √(σ₁²/n₁ + σ₂²/n₂) = √(27.04/25 + 11.56/36) = √(1.0816 + 0.3211) = √1.4027 = 1.1844
Step 2. z = (81 – 76) / 1.1844 = 5 / 1.1844 = 4.22
Step 3. Two-tailed p-value = 2×P(Z > 4.22) ≈ ≈ 0 (reject H₀)
Strong evidence that μ₁ ≠ μ₂.
S24 · P2Abrasive Wear of Two Materials (Pooled t)
Question (Set 24 · P2) An experiment was performed to compare the abrasive wear of two different laminated materials. 12 pieces of material 1 were tested (average wear = 85 units, s₁ = 4) and 10 pieces of material 2 (average = 81, s₂ = 5). Can we conclude at the 0.05 level of significance that the abrasive wear of material 1 exceeds that of material 2 by more than 2 units?
🔍 Formula choice Two groups, σ unknown, s₁=4 and s₂=5 are close (ratio=1.25≤1.4) → Pooled t-test, df=n₁+n₂–2=20. One-tailed upper (H₁: μ₁–μ₂ > 2). Note: Δ₀ = 2, not 0! Sheet → S24/25: Case III (Pooled T, small n, ratio≤1.4)
Step 1. sₚ² = [11(16)+9(25)]/20 = [176+225]/20 = 401/20 = 20.05    sₚ = 4.478
Step 2. ese = 4.478×√(1/12+1/10) = 4.478×0.4282 = 1.917
Step 3. t = [(85–81) – 2] / 1.917 = 2 / 1.917 = 1.04, df=20
Step 4. One-tailed: t0.10,20=1.325. Since 1.04 < 1.325 → p-value > 0.10
Do not reject H₀. Insufficient evidence that wear of material 1 exceeds material 2 by more than 2 units.
S25 · P3Thread Tensile Strength (Manufacturer's Claim)
Question (Set 25 · P3) A manufacturer claims that the average tensile strength of thread A exceeds that of thread B by at least 12 kg. To test this claim, 50 pieces of each were tested. Thread A: x̄₁ = 86.7 kg, s₁ = 6.28. Thread B: x̄₂ = 77.8 kg, s₂ = 5.61. Test the hypothesis H₁: μ₁ – μ₂ < 12 at α = 0.05.
🔍 Formula choice Both n=50 (large) → use z-test. "Exceeds by at least 12" means Δ₀=12. H₁ is one-tailed lower. Trap: Δ₀ = 12, not 0. Subtract 12 in numerator. Sheet → S24/25: Case II (large n, n₁=n₂=50≥30 → Z)
Step 1. ese = √(6.28²/50 + 5.61²/50) = √(0.7892 + 0.6296) = √1.4188 = 1.1912
Step 2. z = [(86.7–77.8) – 12] / 1.1912 = (8.9–12) / 1.1912 = –3.1/1.1912 = –2.60
Step 3. One-tailed lower: p-value = P(Z < –2.60) = 1–0.9953 p-value = 0.0047
Reject H₀. Evidence that the difference is less than 12 kg — manufacturer's claim is not supported.
S25 · P4Tire Brand Wear (Pooled t)

Brand B — x̄₂ = 39,800 km, s₂ = 6,900. Is there reason to believe there is no difference in average wear? Assume normal populations with equal variances.
🔍 Formula choice Two groups, equal variances stated → Pooled t-test. "No difference" → two-tailed, Δ₀=0. Sheet → S24/25: Case III (Pooled T, small n, ratio≤1.4)
Step 1. sₚ² = [11(4500²)+11(6900²)]/22 = [222,750,000+523,710,000]/22 = 33,929,545    sₚ = 5825

Step 3. t0.10,22=1.321. |–0.80| < 1.321 → p-value > 0.20

S27 · P1Nuclear Power Plant — Urban vs Suburban
Question (Set 27 · P1) In a study to estimate the proportion of residents who favor the construction of a nuclear power plant, it is found that 63 of 100 urban residents favor the construction while only 59 of 125 suburban residents are in favor. Is there a significant difference between the proportions of urban and suburban residents who favor construction? Find the p-value.
🔍 Formula choice Two separate groups + proportion outcome (favor/oppose) → two proportions z-test. "Difference" → two-tailed. Must use pooled p̂ₚ for the hypothesis test. Sheet → Two Proportions card → z_obs row, pooled p̂ₚ
Step 1. p̂₁ = 63/100 = 0.63    p̂₂ = 59/125 = 0.472
Step 2. p̂ₚ = (63+59)/(100+125) = 122/225 = 0.5422
Step 3. z = (0.63–0.472) / √(0.5422×0.4578×(1/100+1/125)) = 0.158 / √(0.2483×0.018) = 0.158/0.0669 = 2.36
Step 4. Two-tailed: p-value = 2×P(Z > 2.36) p-value = 0.0164
Reject H₀ at α=0.05. Significant difference between urban and suburban support.

n=6, Σx=111.71, Σy=2.90, Σx²=2895.83, Σy²=1.5317, Σxy=58.835
S_xx = 2895.83 – (111.71)²/6 = 2895.83 – 2079.28 = 644.94 (use calculator)
Actually from Python: S_xx=644.94, S_yy=0.2555, S_xy=9.9218
Cov = S_xy/(n–1) = 9.9218/5 = 1.984
r = S_xy/√(S_xx·S_yy) = 9.9218/√(644.94×0.2555) = 9.9218/12.843 r = 0.773
R² = r² = 0.773² R² = 0.597   (59.7% of variation in thickness explained by stiffness)
(b) Slope b₁ and Intercept b₀
b₁ = S_xy/S_xx = 9.9218/644.94 b₁ = 0.0154
x̄ = 111.71/6 = 18.618    ȳ = 2.90/6 = 0.4833

(c) Residual at x = 6.92
ŷ = 0.1969 + 0.0154(6.92) = 0.1969 + 0.1066 = 0.3035

(d) Predicted at x = 20

t = r√[(n–2)/(1–r²)] = 0.773×√[4/(1–0.5973)] = 0.773×√(4/0.4027) = 0.773×3.151 = 2.44, df=4
