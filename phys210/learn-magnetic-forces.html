1. A field acts on moving charges

F⃗B=qv⃗×B⃗,FB=∣q∣vBsin⁡θ.\vec F_B=q\vec v\times\vec B,\qquad F_B=|q|vB\sin\theta.FB​=qv×B,FB​=∣q∣vBsinθ. B is in teslas (T), v is particle velocity, and θ is the angle between v and B. Point right-hand fingers along v and curl toward B to obtain force for positive q; reverse for negative q. Magnetic force is perpendicular to velocity, so it does no work on a point charge. Dots represent out of the page and crosses into it.

2. Circular motion and flux

For velocity perpendicular to a uniform field, ∣q∣vB=mv2/r|q|vB=mv^2/r∣q∣vB=mv2/r, so r=mv/(∣q∣B)r=mv/(|q|B)r=mv/(∣q∣B) and period T=2πm/(∣q∣B)T=2\pi m/(|q|B)T=2πm/(∣q∣B). A parallel velocity component produces helical motion instead. Magnetic flux is ΦB=∫B⃗⋅dA⃗\Phi_B=\int\vec B\cdot d\vec AΦB​=∫B⋅dA in webers (Wb). It uses the angle with the surface normal.

3. Wires, loops, and torque

A straight wire segment has F⃗=Iℓ⃗×B⃗\vec F=I\vec\ell\times\vec BF=Iℓ×B, where ℓ points with conventional current. For a curved wire, integrate I dℓ⃗×B⃗I\,d\vec\ell\times\vec BIdℓ×B. A planar N-turn loop has magnetic moment μ⃗=NIA⃗\vec\mu=NI\vec Aμ​=NIA and torque τ⃗=μ⃗×B⃗\vec\tau=\vec\mu\times\vec Bτ=μ​×B; its energy is U=−μ⃗⋅B⃗U=-\vec\mu\cdot\vec BU=−μ​⋅B. The area direction follows a right-hand curl along current.
