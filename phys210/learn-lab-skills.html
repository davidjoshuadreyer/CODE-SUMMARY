PHYS 210 / ELECTRICITY AND MAGNETISM

Lab skills: uncertainty, graphs, and reports

LESSON 13 OF 13 · WEEKS 1–14 · TEXTBOOK Course lab manual

1. Write measurements honestly

Record a value, units, and its uncertainty, such as (12.4±0.1) cm(12.4\pm0.1)\,\mathrm{cm}(12.4±0.1)cm. Distinguish instrument resolution, random scatter, and systematic offsets. Repeat readings to characterize scatter; repeated readings do not automatically remove calibration bias. Preserve raw measurements and convert units in a separate calculation.

2. Propagate uncertainty under stated assumptions

For independent small random uncertainties in f(x1,…,xn)f(x_1,\ldots,x_n)f(x1​,…,xn​), uf=∑i(∂f∂xiui)2.u_f=\sqrt{\sum_i\left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_i}u_i\right)^2}.uf​=i∑​(∂xi​∂f​ui​)2​. Here u_i is the standard uncertainty of x_i. For a product or quotient, relative uncertainties combine in quadrature under these assumptions. A worst-case bound instead adds absolute contributions. Use the convention specified by your lab handout; these answer different questions.

3. Use graphs to test a model

Put units on both axes, choose a model, and determine slope and intercept with their units. For V versus I in an ohmic resistor, slope is R in V/A = Ω. Discuss residuals and uncertainty, not just whether a line looks straight. A report should connect the objective, apparatus and procedure, raw observations, analysis, and conclusion. Explain whether results support the model within uncertainty, and identify specific error mechanisms.
