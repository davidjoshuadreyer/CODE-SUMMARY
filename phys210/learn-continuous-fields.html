1. Replace a sum with an integral

Choose a source coordinate and a small charge dqdqdq. For a line, dq=λ dsdq=\lambda\,dsdq=λds; for a surface, dq=σ dAdq=\sigma\,dAdq=σdA; for a volume, dq=ρ dVdq=\rho\,dVdq=ρdV. The densities have units C/m, C/m², and C/m³. If density varies, keep that dependence inside the integral. 2. Let symmetry remove components

For a uniformly charged ring of radius a and total charge Q, observed distance x along its axis, sideways components cancel in opposite pairs. Every piece is distance x2+a2\sqrt{x^2+a^2}x2+a2​ away. Projection gives Ex=kQx(x2+a2)3/2.E_x=\frac{kQx}{(x^2+a^2)^{3/2}}.Ex​=(x2+a2)3/2kQx​. The sign includes the signs of Q and x. The field at the centre is zero, even though the electric potential need not be zero.

3. Build a disk from rings

A disk of radius R and uniform surface density σ contains rings with dq=σ(2πs ds)dq=\sigma(2\pi s\,ds)dq=σ(2πsds). On its positive axis at x > 0, Ex=σ2ε0(1−xx2+R2).E_x=\frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0}\left(1-\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+R^2}}\right).Ex​=2ε0​σ​(1−x2+R2​x​). Here s is the ring integration radius and ε0=8.854×10−12 C2/(N m2)\varepsilon_0=8.854\times10^{-12}\,\mathrm{C^2/(N\,m^2)}ε0​=8.854×10−12C2/(Nm2). Near a large disk this approaches the infinite-sheet field; far away use total charge Q=σπR2Q=\sigma\pi R^2Q=σπR2 to recover kQ/x2kQ/x^2kQ/x2.

Worked example

Given / Find

A ring has Q = +1.00 µC and a = 0.100 m. Find the field x = 0.100 m along its axis.

Identify: use ring symmetry. Set up: only axial components survive. Execute: Ex=kQx/(x2+a2)3/2=3.18×105 N/CE_x=kQx/(x^2+a^2)^{3/2}=3.18\times10^5\,\mathrm{N/C}Ex​=kQx/(x2+a2)3/2=3.18×105N/C. Evaluate: it points away from the positive ring; the formula gives zero at x = 0.

What changes if a uniform ring has negative charge?
