Textbook Capacitor

Measure how capacitance changes with plate area and gap, then find the dielectric constant of paper.

Based on the saved lab manual, pp. L4-1–L4-4. New D2L announcements have not been checked. Confirm this is your assigned experiment.

Before the lab

Bring a textbook with at least 500 pages. The manual also lists a Circuit Test DCM 200 capacitance meter, aluminum foil, micrometer, scissors, rulers and a paper cutter.

Meter/range: __________

  • Keep the foil plates flat and aligned. Connect the meter clips to small foil tabs.
  • Record the meter unit and estimate capacitance uncertainty from the changing readings.
  • Measure actual dimensions and their uncertainties. Suggested dimensions are not measured data.
  • Count sheets, not printed pages: a difference of 200 page numbers means 100 sheets.

C is capacitance, A is overlapping area, d is the gap, and κ is the dielectric constant (no units).

A · Paper thickness

  1. Select 100 sheets and record the start and end page numbers.
  2. Measure total thickness T with the micrometer. The manual gives ±10 μm, or ±0.010 mm.
  3. Calculate thickness t of one sheet and its uncertainty.

B · Fixed area, changing gap

  1. Cut two matching foil squares; 20 cm × 20 cm is suggested. Measure length l, width w and their uncertainties.
  2. Insert and align the plates between textbook pages. Connect the clips to the tabs and record C and ΔC.
  3. Move one plate to increase the gap. Collect six different gaps; the suggested range is 20–200 sheets.

C · Fixed gap, changing area

  1. Keep a fixed gap; 100 sheets is suggested. Record the page positions and use them for every reading.
  2. Measure C using the original plates.
  3. Remove both plates and trim or fold them to matching smaller sizes. Reinsert at the same pages and measure C again.
  4. Collect six areas. Suggested sizes are 20 × 20, 18 × 18, 16 × 16, 14 × 14, 12 × 12 and 10 × 10 cm. Record actual dimensions.

Graphs and calculations

Convert to SI units: cm × 10⁻² → m; mm × 10⁻³ → m; pF × 10⁻¹² → F; nF × 10⁻⁹ → F; μF × 10⁻⁶ → F. Apply the same conversion to uncertainties.

Use the instructor’s Excel template. Include uncertainty on both axes, at least for the first and last points. Each graph should be approximately straight with positive slope. Explain any excluded readings.

Use the fixed area from B and fixed gap from C. Treat ε₀ as a constant. Keep extra digits until the end; round final uncertainties to one significant figure, then results to the same decimal place.
