Electric flux and Gauss’s law

Distinguish field strength from field passing through a surface.

Given: A point charge Q = +3.00 nC at the center of a spherical Gaussian surface of radius r = 0.200 m.

Find: The total outward electric flux and the field on the sphere.

Define the variables

Q: enclosed charge (C); r: sphere radius (m); A = 4πr²: sphere area (m²); Φ: flux (N m²/C); ε₀ = 8.854 × 10⁻¹² C²/(N m²); E: field magnitude (N/C).

k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m²/C²; E is field magnitude (N/C). Subscripts identify components.

Worked example

  1. Choose the surface

    Spherical symmetry makes E constant on the sphere and parallel to every outward area vector.

  2. Apply Gauss’s law

    Φ=∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=Qε0=339 N m2/C\Phi=\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec A=\frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}=339\ \mathrm{N\,m^2/C}Φ=∮E⋅dA=ε0​Q​=339 Nm2/C

  3. Find the field

    E(4πr2)=Qε0⇒E=Q4πε0r2=674 N/CE(4\pi r^2)=\frac{Q}{\varepsilon_0}\quad\Rightarrow\quad E=\frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_0r^2}=674\ \mathrm{N/C}E(4πr2)=ε0​Q​⇒E=4πε0​r2Q​=674 N/C

  4. Give the direction

    The positive enclosed charge produces a radially outward field.
Check your answer

Increasing the radius reduces E but leaves total flux unchanged. Gauss’s law always holds; pulling E outside the integral requires symmetry.

Try it yourself

If the radius doubles, what happens to E and Φ?

Show the worked answer

The area becomes four times larger. E falls to one quarter (about 169 N/C); Φ stays 339 N m²/C.
