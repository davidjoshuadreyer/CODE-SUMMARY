PHYS 210 / ELECTRICITY AND MAGNETISM

Faraday’s law, Lenz’s law, and motional emf

LESSON 11 OF 13 · WEEKS 12–13 · TEXTBOOK 29.1–29.5

1. Start with changing flux

An N-turn coil has induced emf E=−NdΦBdt,ΦB=∫B⃗⋅dA⃗.\mathcal E=-N\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt},\qquad\Phi_B=\int\vec B\cdot d\vec A.E=−NdtdΦB​​,ΦB​=∫B⋅dA. A change in field, area, or orientation may change flux. Emf is in volts; it exists even if an open circuit prevents current. For a uniform B and flat coil, ΦB=BAcos⁡θ\Phi_B=BA\cos\thetaΦB​=BAcosθ, with θ measured from the area normal.

2. Find direction separately

Choose a surface normal and its right-hand positive loop direction. Lenz’s law says the induced current’s field opposes the change in flux, not necessarily the existing field. If outward flux is increasing, induced field is inward, giving clockwise current. If outward flux is decreasing, induced field is outward, giving counterclockwise current.

3. Moving conductors and induced fields

A conducting rod of length ℓ moving perpendicular to itself and uniform B has emf magnitude BℓvB\ell vBℓv. Direction follows qv⃗×B⃗q\vec v\times\vec Bqv×B on positive charges. In a closed resistive circuit use I=E/RI=\mathcal E/RI=E/R. A changing magnetic field can also produce a circulating electric field in empty space: ∮E⃗⋅dℓ⃗=−dΦB/dt\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec\ell=-d\Phi_B/dt∮E⋅dℓ=−dΦB​/dt. This induced field is not described globally by a single-valued electrostatic potential.

Worked example

Given / Find

A 100-turn coil of area 0.0100 m² has outward perpendicular B increasing from 0.100 to 0.300 T in 0.200 s. Find emf magnitude and induced current direction.

Identify: flux increases outward. Set up: ∣E∣=NA∣ΔB/Δt∣|\mathcal E|=NA|\Delta B/\Delta t|∣E∣=NA∣ΔB/Δt∣. Execute: ∣E∣=1.00 V|\mathcal E|=1.00\,\mathrm V∣E∣=1.00V. Evaluate: the induced field is inward, so current is clockwise as viewed with outward normal toward you.
