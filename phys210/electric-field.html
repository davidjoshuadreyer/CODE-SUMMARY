Electric fields: start with direction

Turn charges and distances into a field vector.

Given: A source charge Q = +2.00 µC. A point P is 0.300 m to its right.

Find: The electric field at P.

Define the variables

Q: source charge (C); r: source-to-point distance (m); E: field magnitude (N/C); k: Coulomb’s constant, 8.99 × 10⁹ N m²/C². A positive x direction points right.

k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m²/C²; E is field magnitude (N/C). Subscripts identify components.

Worked example

  1. Draw the direction

    The field of positive charge points away from it. At P, that means right.

  2. Convert the charge

    Q=2.00×10−6 CQ=2.00\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm CQ=2.00×10−6 C

  3. Apply Coulomb’s law

    E=k∣Q∣r2=(8.99×109)(2.00×10−6)0.3002=2.00×105 N/CE=\frac{k|Q|}{r^2}=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(2.00\times10^{-6})}{0.300^2}=2.00\times10^5\ \mathrm{N/C}E=r2k∣Q∣​=0.3002(8.99×109)(2.00×10−6)​=2.00×105 N/C

  4. State the answer

    The field is 2.00 × 10⁵ N/C to the right. This is a field, not a force: for a test charge q, multiply the field vector by q to find force.
Check your answer

Doubling the distance divides the field by four. A negative source would reverse the direction.

Try it yourself

What changes if the source is −2.00 µC?

Show the worked answer

The magnitude stays 2.00 × 10⁵ N/C. The field points left, toward the negative source.
