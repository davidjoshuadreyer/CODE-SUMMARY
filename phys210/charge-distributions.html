Continuous charge: one piece at a time

Choose dq, project the field, and integrate.

Given: A uniformly charged ring: radius a = 0.100 m, charge Q = +1.00 µC. P lies x = 0.100 m along its axis.

Find: The net electric field at P.

Define the variables

a: ring radius (m); x: axial distance (m); r = √(x² + a²): distance from any ring piece to P; dq: tiny charge (C); dEₓ: its axial field component (N/C).

k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m²/C²; E is field magnitude (N/C). Subscripts identify components.

Worked example

  1. Use symmetry

    Opposite ring pieces have equal and opposite sideways components. Only the axial components add.

  2. Project one piece

    dEx=k dqr2xr,r=x2+a2dE_x=\frac{k\,dq}{r^2}\frac{x}{r},\qquad r=\sqrt{x^2+a^2}dEx​=r2kdq​rx​,r=x2+a2​

  3. Add the ring

    Ex=kx(x2+a2)3/2∫dq=kQx(x2+a2)3/2E_x=\frac{kx}{(x^2+a^2)^{3/2}}\int dq=\frac{kQx}{(x^2+a^2)^{3/2}}Ex​=(x2+a2)3/2kx​∫dq=(x2+a2)3/2kQx​

  4. Substitute

    Ex=(8.99×109)(1.00×10−6)(0.100)(0.1002+0.1002)3/2=3.18×105 N/CE_x=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(1.00\times10^{-6})(0.100)}{(0.100^2+0.100^2)^{3/2}}=3.18\times10^5\ \mathrm{N/C}Ex​=(0.1002+0.1002)3/2(8.99×109)(1.00×10−6)(0.100)​=3.18×105 N/CThe field points along +x, away from the positive ring.
Check your answer

At the center, x = 0 and the field is zero. Far away, the formula approaches kQ/x².
