RC charging and discharging

1. Use the time constant

A resistor R and capacitor C have time constant τ=RC\tau=RCτ=RC (seconds). Capacitor voltage cannot change instantaneously under finite current. When initially uncharged and connected in series to ideal emf E\mathcal EE, q(t)=CE(1−e−t/RC),I(t)=ERe−t/RC.q(t)=C\mathcal E(1-e^{-t/RC}),\quad I(t)=\frac{\mathcal E}{R}e^{-t/RC}.q(t)=CE(1−e−t/RC),I(t)=RE​e−t/RC. Here t is elapsed time after switching.

2. Discharge and general starting states

For discharge from initial charge Q₀ through R, q(t)=Q0e−t/RCq(t)=Q_0e^{-t/RC}q(t)=Q0​e−t/RC. If positive current is defined as flowing onto the initially positive capacitor plate, I=dq/dt=−Q0e−t/RC/(RC)I=dq/dt=-Q_0e^{-t/RC}/(RC)I=dq/dt=−Q0​e−t/RC/(RC). The negative sign shows discharge. More generally, VC(t)=Vf+(Vi−Vf)e−t/τV_C(t)=V_f+(V_i-V_f)e^{-t/\tau}VC​(t)=Vf​+(Vi​−Vf​)e−t/τ, where V_i and V_f are initial and final capacitor voltages.

3. Check limits before calculating

At t = 0, the initially uncharged capacitor has zero voltage. As t approaches infinity during DC charging, I tends to zero and V_C tends to the source voltage. At one time constant it has completed 63.2% of the change; after five it has completed about 99.3%. In a larger network R is the resistance seen by the capacitor with independent sources deactivated.
