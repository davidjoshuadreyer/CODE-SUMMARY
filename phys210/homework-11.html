Question 127.37 · Lift-off of a conducting bar

Bar L = 0.500 m, mass 0.750 kg, B = 0.450 T into the page; current runs rightward. Series resistance starts at 25.0 Ω, then falls to 2.0 Ω. Find maximum battery voltage before lift-off, then initial acceleration after resistance falls.

I′=VmaxR′=816.72.0=408.3 AFB=I′LB=(408.3)(0.500)(0.450)=91.875 Na=FB−mgm=91.875−7.3500.750=112.7 m/s2\begin{gathered}I'=\frac{V_{max}}{R'}=\frac{816.7}{2.0}=408.3\ \mathrm A\\F_B=I'LB=(408.3)(0.500)(0.450)=91.875\ \mathrm N\\a=\frac{F_B-mg}{m}=\frac{91.875-7.350}{0.750}=112.7\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\end{gathered}I′=R′Vmax​​=2.0816.7​=408.3 AFB​=I′LB=(408.3)(0.500)(0.450)=91.875 Na=mFB​−mg​=0.75091.875−7.350​=112.7 m/s2​

Question 227.41 · Torque about a loop hinge

Rectangle abcd: vertical ab = cd = 0.200 m; horizontal bc = da = 0.350 m. Clockwise current 5.00 A; B = 1.20 T rightward; hinge along left side ab. Find force directions, identify which force torques the hinge, and calculate torque.

Original questionOpen the original D2L handout

Parallel sides feel zero magnetic force; a force at the hinge has zero lever arm.

Find the four forces

F=IL×BFab=Fcd=ILB=(5.00)(0.200)(1.20)=1.20 NFbc=Fda=ILBsin⁡0∘=0\begin{gathered}\mathbf F=I\mathbf L\times\mathbf B\\F_{ab}=F_{cd}=ILB=(5.00)(0.200)(1.20)=1.20\ \mathrm N\\F_{bc}=F_{da}=ILB\sin0^\circ=0\end{gathered}F=IL×BFab​=Fcd​=ILB=(5.00)(0.200)(1.20)=1.20 NFbc​=Fda​=ILBsin0∘=0​

Find torque about the hinge

τab=0(force at the hinge)τcd=Fcd(0.350)=(1.20)(0.350)=0.420 N m\begin{gathered}\tau_{ab}=0\quad\text{(force at the hinge)}\\\tau_{cd}=F_{cd}(0.350)=(1.20)(0.350)=0.420\ \mathrm{N\,m}\end{gathered}τab​=0(force at the hinge)τcd​=Fcd​(0.350)=(1.20)(0.350)=0.420 Nm​

Force on ab is into the page; force on cd is out of the page. The other two sides are parallel to B.

Final answer

Only cd torques the hinge: 1.20 N outward; torque 0.420 N m.

The right side cd swings out of the page. Net force is zero, but the force couple produces nonzero torque.

Question 327.42 · Rotating a current loop

Loop 0.220 m × 0.350 m, I = 1.95 A counterclockwise, B = 1.50 T into page. Initially loop plane is perpendicular to B; then rotate 30° with left side outward and right side inward. Find net force and torque before and after rotation.

Original questionOpen the original D2L handout

A closed loop in uniform B has zero net force; torque is μ × B.

Before rotation

A=(0.220)(0.350)=0.0770 m2μ=IA=(1.95)(0.0770)=0.15015 A m2Fnet=0,τ=μBsin⁡180∘=0\begin{gathered}A=(0.220)(0.350)=0.0770\ \mathrm{m^2}\\\mu=IA=(1.95)(0.0770)=0.15015\ \mathrm{A\,m^2}\\F_{net}=0,\quad\tau=\mu B\sin180^\circ=0\end{gathered}A=(0.220)(0.350)=0.0770 m2μ=IA=(1.95)(0.0770)=0.15015 Am2Fnet​=0,τ=μBsin180∘=0​

After rotation

θ=180∘−30∘=150∘τ=μBsin⁡θ=(0.15015)(1.50)sin⁡150∘τ=0.113 N m,Fnet=0\begin{gathered}\theta=180^\circ-30^\circ=150^\circ\\\tau=\mu B\sin\theta=(0.15015)(1.50)\sin150^\circ\\\tau=0.113\ \mathrm{N\,m},\quad F_{net}=0\end{gathered}θ=180∘−30∘=150∘τ=μBsinθ=(0.15015)(1.50)sin150∘τ=0.113 Nm,Fnet​=0​

Final answer

Both net forces zero; torque 0 initially, then 0.113 N m continuing the rotation.

The initial magnetic moment is opposite B (unstable equilibrium). After the stated rotation the torque continues that rotation, with left side moving further out.

Question 427.56 · Cyclotron energy limit

Cyclotron B = 0.85 T, maximum orbit radius 0.40 m. Proton q = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. Alpha q = 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. Find proton maximum energy in J/eV, period, B needed to double energy, and alpha maximum energy at 0.85 T.

Original questionOpen the original D2L handout

Use the orbit radius to obtain speed, then kinetic energy.

Find proton energy and period

Kmax=q2B2R22m=(1.60×10−19)2(0.85)2(0.40)22(1.67×10−27)Kp=8.86×10−13 J=8.86×10−131.60×10−19 eV=5.54 MeVTp=2πmqB=2π(1.67×10−27)(1.60×10−19)(0.85)=7.72×10−8 s\begin{gathered}K_{max}=\frac{q^2B^2R^2}{2m}\\=\frac{(1.60\times10^{-19})^2(0.85)^2(0.40)^2}{2(1.67\times10^{-27})}\\K_p=8.86\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm J=\frac{8.86\times10^{-13}}{1.60\times10^{-19}}\ \mathrm{eV}=5.54\ \mathrm{MeV}\\T_p=\frac{2\pi m}{qB}=\frac{2\pi(1.67\times10^{-27})}{(1.60\times10^{-19})(0.85)}=7.72\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm s\end{gathered}Kmax​=2mq2B2R2​=2(1.67×10−27)(1.60×10−19)2(0.85)2(0.40)2​Kp​=8.86×10−13 J=1.60×10−198.86×10−13​ eV=5.54 MeVTp​=qB2πm​=(1.60×10−19)(0.85)2π(1.67×10−27)​=7.72×10−8 s​

Double the proton energy

K∝B2⇒B′=2B=2(0.85)=1.20 T\begin{gathered}K\propto B^2\quad\Rightarrow\quad B'=\sqrt2B=\sqrt2(0.85)=1.20\ \mathrm T\end{gathered}K∝B2⇒B′=2​B=2​(0.85)=1.20 T​

Find the alpha-particle energy

Kα=(3.20×10−19)2(0.85)2(0.40)22(6.64×10−27)Kα=8.91×10−13 J=5.57 MeV\begin{gathered}K_\alpha=\frac{(3.20\times10^{-19})^2(0.85)^2(0.40)^2}{2(6.64\times10^{-27})}\\K_\alpha=8.91\times10^{-13}\ \mathrm J=5.57\ \mathrm{MeV}\end{gathered}Kα​=2(6.64×10−27)(3.20×10−19)2(0.85)2(0.40)2​Kα​=8.91×10−13 J=5.57 MeV​

Final answer

Proton 5.54 MeV; period 77.2 ns; doubling requires 1.20 T; alpha 5.57 MeV.

The alpha energy is almost the same: its charge squared is four times greater and mass approximately four times greater. Nonrelativistic model.

Question 527.65 · Rail-gun acceleration

A bar of mass m and length L bridges horizontal rails in uniform vertical field B, carrying constant current I across the bar as shown in the handout. Ignore friction and gravity for the motion. Derive force and distance from rest to speed v; evaluate for 11.2 km/s, B = 0.80 T, I = 2.0 × 10³ A, m = 25 kg, L = 0.50 m.

Original questionOpen the original D2L handout

Magnetic force is constant, so use constant acceleration or work–energy.

Find force and distance symbolically

FB=ILB,a=ILBmv2=2ad⇒d=mv22ILB\begin{gathered}F_B=ILB,\quad a=\frac{ILB}{m}\\v^2=2ad\quad\Rightarrow\quad d=\frac{mv^2}{2ILB}\end{gathered}FB​=ILB,a=mILB​v2=2ad⇒d=2ILBmv2​​

FB=(2.0×103)(0.50)(0.80)=800 Na=800/25=32 m/s2d=25(11.2×103)22(2.0×103)(0.50)(0.80)d=1.96×106 m≈1960 km\begin{gathered}F_B=(2.0\times10^3)(0.50)(0.80)=800\ \mathrm N\\a=800/25=32\ \mathrm{m/s^2}\\d=\frac{25(11.2\times10^3)^2}{2(2.0\times10^3)(0.50)(0.80)}\\d=1.96\times10^6\ \mathrm m\approx1960\ \mathrm{km}\end{gathered}FB​=(2.0×103)(0.50)(0.80)=800 Na=800/25=32 m/s2d=2(2.0×103)(0.50)(0.80)25(11.2×103)2​d=1.96×106 m≈1960 km​

Final answer

F = ILB along rails; d = mv²/(2ILB); example distance 1.96 × 10⁶ m.

Force points along the rails away from the source for the shown current. The 1960 km distance illustrates the limits of this simplified launch model.

Question 627.69 · Suspended loop in equilibrium
