21.1 · Counting excess electrons

A small lead sphere has mass 8.00 g and net charge −3.20 × 10⁻⁹ C. Find (a) the number of excess electrons and (b) the excess electrons per lead atom. Lead has atomic number 82 and atomic mass 207 g/mol.

Original homework ↗ · Instructor solution key ↗, PDF page 1 (Camosun login).

Given
m = 8.00 g; Q = −3.20 × 10⁻⁹ C; M = 207 g/mol; Nₐ = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.
Find
Nₑ, the count of excess electrons; Nₑ/N, excess electrons per lead atom.
Variables
m: sphere mass; Q: net charge; M: molar mass; Nₐ: Avogadro constant; e: positive elementary charge; Nₑ: excess-electron count; N: lead-atom count; n: amount in moles.

Help as you work

Hint

First divide total charge magnitude by the charge of one electron. Count lead atoms separately using m/M.

1. Identify
The negative net charge means electrons were added. We count only the excess, not all electrons already in the atoms.
2. Set up
Ne=∣Q∣/eN_e=|Q|/eNe​=∣Q∣/e, n=m/Mn=m/Mn=m/M, and N=nNAN=nN_AN=nNA​. Keep mass and molar-mass units consistent.
3. Execute
Ne=3.20×10−91.602×10−19=2.00×1010N_e=\frac{3.20\times10^{-9}}{1.602\times10^{-19}}=2.00\times10^{10}Ne​=1.602×10−193.20×10−9​=2.00×1010 n=8.00207=0.03865 mol,N=2.328×1022n=\frac{8.00}{207}=0.03865\ \mathrm{mol},\quad N=2.328\times10^{22}n=2078.00​=0.03865 mol,N=2.328×1022 NeN=8.59×10−13 excess electrons per atom\frac{N_e}{N}=8.59\times10^{-13}\ \text{excess electrons per atom}NNe​​=8.59×10−13 excess electrons per atom

A large number of excess electrons still represents a tiny fraction of the atoms. The atomic number 82 is not needed for counting atoms from molar mass.
Final answer

21.3 · Proton and electron acceleration

A proton and an electron are released from rest 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m apart. Find the initial acceleration of each particle.

Original homework ↗ · Instructor solution key ↗, PDF page 1 (Camosun login).

Given
r = 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ m; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m²/C²; mₚ = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg; mₑ = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.
Find
The initial acceleration magnitude and direction for each particle.
Variables
r: initial separation; e: elementary charge magnitude; k: Coulomb constant; mₚ and mₑ: proton and electron masses; F: electric-force magnitude; aₚ and aₑ: acceleration magnitudes.

Help as you work

Hint

Both particles feel the same force magnitude. Divide that force by each particle’s own mass.

1. Identify
Opposite charges attract. Apply Coulomb’s law, then Newton’s second law to each particle.
2. Set up
F=ke2r2,ap=Fmp,ae=FmeF=\frac{ke^2}{r^2},\qquad a_p=\frac{F}{m_p},\quad a_e=\frac{F}{m_e}F=r2ke2​,ap​=mp​F​,ae​=me​F​
3. Execute
F=(8.99×109)(1.60×10−19)2(2.0×10−10)2=5.75×10−9 NF=\frac{(8.99\times10^9)(1.60\times10^{-19})^2}{(2.0\times10^{-10})^2}=5.75\times10^{-9}\ \mathrm NF=(2.0×10−10)2(8.99×109)(1.60×10−19)2​=5.75×10−9 N ap=3.4×1018 m/s2,ae=6.3×1021 m/s2a_p=3.4\times10^{18}\ \mathrm{m/s^2},\qquad a_e=6.3\times10^{21}\ \mathrm{m/s^2}ap​=3.4×1018 m/s2,ae​=6.3×1021 m/s2Each acceleration points toward the other particle.
4. Evaluate
Equal and opposite forces do not imply equal accelerations: the electron has much less mass. These are initial values; force changes as separation changes.
Final answer

Proton: 3.4 × 10¹⁸ m/s²; electron: 6.3 × 10²¹ m/s², each toward the other.

21.9 · Solve for unknown charges

Two positively charged plastic spheres are 15.0 cm apart and repel with force 0.220 N. Find each charge (a) if the charges are equal and (b) if one charge is four times the other.

Original homework ↗ · Instructor solution key ↗, PDF page 2 (Camosun login).

Given
r = 0.150 m; F = 0.220 N; k = 8.988 × 10⁹ N m²/C².
Find
q₁ and q₂ for equal charges, then for q₂ = 4q₁.
Variables
r: centre-to-centre separation (point-charge model); F: force magnitude on either sphere; q₁, q₂: positive charges; k: Coulomb constant.

Help as you work

Hint

Substitute the charge relationship into F = kq₁q₂/r² before solving.

1. Identify
Use Coulomb’s law. The force is given, so solve backwards for the charges.
2. Set up
q1q2=Fr2kq_1q_2=\frac{Fr^2}{k}q1​q2​=kFr2​This fixes the product, not each charge separately. The extra relationship makes the solution unique.
3. Execute
For (a), q1=q2=qq_1=q_2=qq1​=q2​=q: q=Fr2k=7.42×10−7 C.q=\sqrt{\frac{Fr^2}{k}}=7.42\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm C.q=kFr2​​=7.42×10−7 C. For (b), q2=4q1q_2=4q_1q2​=4q1​: q1=Fr24k=3.71×10−7 C,q2=1.48×10−6 C.q_1=\sqrt{\frac{Fr^2}{4k}}=3.71\times10^{-7}\ \mathrm C,\qquad q_2=1.48\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm C.q1​=4kFr2​​=3.71×10−7 C,q2​=1.48×10−6 C.
4. Evaluate
In part (b), one charge halves and the other doubles relative to part (a). Their product and the force stay the same.
Final answer

(a) 7.42 × 10⁻⁷ C each. (b) 3.71 × 10⁻⁷ C and 1.48 × 10⁻⁶ C.

21.63 · Two suspended charged spheres

Two spheres of mass 15.0 g hang from one point on threads of length 1.20 m. Equal negative charges make each thread tilt 25.0° from vertical. (a) Draw each free-body diagram. (b) Find the charge magnitude. (c) Shorten both threads to 0.600 m, keeping the charges unchanged; find the new angle.

Original homework ↗ · Instructor solution key ↗, PDF page 1–2 (Camosun login).

Given
m = 0.0150 kg for each sphere; L = 1.20 m initially; θ = 25.0°; g = 9.80 m/s²; final L = 0.600 m.
Find
Forces on each sphere, charge magnitude |q|, and the final angle θ.
Variables
m: one sphere’s mass; L: one thread’s length; θ: angle from vertical; r = 2L sin θ: sphere separation; T: tension; Fₑ: electric repulsion; q: one sphere’s charge; g: gravitational acceleration; k = 8.988 × 10⁹ N m²/C².

Help as you work

Hint

Resolve tension into vertical and horizontal components. Dividing the balance equations eliminates T.

1. Identify
Each sphere is at rest: its net force is zero. Weight acts down, tension points along the thread toward the support, and electric repulsion points away from the other sphere.
2. Set up
Tcos⁡θ=mgTsin⁡θ=FeFe=mgtan⁡θ\begin{aligned}T\cos\theta&=mg\\T\sin\theta&=F_e\\F_e&=mg\tan\theta\end{aligned}TcosθTsinθFe​​=mg=Fe​=mgtanθ​ The separation is r=2Lsin⁡θr=2L\sin\thetar=2Lsinθ, not LLL.
3. Execute
∣q∣=2Lsin⁡θmgtan⁡θk=2.80×10−6 C.|q|=2L\sin\theta\sqrt{\frac{mg\tan\theta}{k}}=2.80\times10^{-6}\ \mathrm C.∣q∣=2Lsinθkmgtanθ​​=2.80×10−6 C. For the shorter threads, keep this charge and solve sin⁡2θtan⁡θ=kq24L2mg≈0.333.\sin^2\theta\tan\theta=\frac{kq^2}{4L^2mg}\approx0.333.sin2θtanθ=4L2mgkq2​≈0.333. Trial angles: 39.4° gives 0.3309, 39.5° gives 0.3335, and 39.6° gives 0.3361. Thus θ≈39.5∘\theta\approx39.5^\circθ≈39.5∘.
4. Evaluate
Shorter threads bring the spheres closer at the old angle, increasing repulsion. The new angle must increase. Both actual charges are negative; 2.80 µC is the magnitude.
Final answer

|q| = 2.80 µC; θ ≈ 39.5° after shortening.

21.65 · Infer a field from a hanging ball

A 12.3 g ball carrying −1.11 µC hangs on a 28.6 cm string from a vertical wall. The string makes 17.4° with the wall in a uniform horizontal electric field. Find the field magnitude and direction.

Original homework ↗ · Instructor solution key ↗, PDF page 3 (Camosun login).

Given
m = 0.0123 kg; q = −1.11 × 10⁻⁶ C; L = 0.286 m; θ = 17.4° from vertical; g = 9.80 m/s².
Find
E, the horizontal field magnitude, and its direction.
Variables
m: ball mass; q: ball charge; L: string length; θ: angle from the vertical wall; T: tension; Fₑ: electric force magnitude; E: electric-field magnitude.

Help as you work

Hint

The electric force points away from the wall. Because the charge is negative, the field points the opposite way.

1. Identify
The ball is in equilibrium. Use horizontal and vertical force balance, then Fₑ = |q|E.
2. Set up
Tcos⁡θ=mgTsin⁡θ=FeFe=mgtan⁡θ\begin{aligned}T\cos\theta&=mg\\T\sin\theta&=F_e\\F_e&=mg\tan\theta\end{aligned}TcosθTsinθFe​​=mg=Fe​=mgtanθ​

Fe=(0.0123)(9.80)tan⁡17.4∘=3.78×10−2 NF_e=(0.0123)(9.80)\tan17.4^\circ=3.78\times10^{-2}\ \mathrm NFe​=(0.0123)(9.80)tan17.4∘=3.78×10−2 N E=Fe∣q∣=3.41×104 N/C.E=\frac{F_e}{|q|}=3.41\times10^4\ \mathrm{N/C}.E=∣q∣Fe​​=3.41×104 N/C.Force points away from the wall; the field points toward the wall.

21.78 · Deflection between charged plates

An object enters horizontal plates at 5.00 × 10³ m/s. The plates are 26.0 cm long and produce a downward field of 800 N/C; outside them the field is zero. A collecting screen is 56.0 cm beyond the plate end. Total downward deflection at the screen is 1.25 cm. Ignore gravity and air resistance. Find q/m.

Original homework ↗ · Instructor solution key ↗, PDF page 4 (Camosun login).

Given
v₀ = 5.00 × 10³ m/s; ℓ = 0.260 m; D = 0.560 m; E = 800 N/C; d = 0.0125 m.
Find
q/m, including its sign.
Variables
v₀: constant horizontal speed; ℓ: plate length; D: distance after plates to screen; d: total downward displacement; E: downward field; t₁, t₂: times inside and after plates; aᵧ: downward acceleration inside plates; q: charge; m: mass.

Help as you work

Hint

Split the motion into two stages: accelerated motion inside the plates, then constant vertical velocity outside.

1. Identify
Take x to the right and y downward. The downward deflection in a downward field implies positive charge. Horizontal speed stays constant.
2. Set up
t1=ℓ/v0,t2=D/v0,ay=(q/m)E.t_1=\ell/v_0,\quad t_2=D/v_0,\quad a_y=(q/m)E.t1​=ℓ/v0​,t2​=D/v0​,ay​=(q/m)E. d=12ayt12+(ayt1)t2.d=\tfrac12 a_y t_1^2+(a_y t_1)t_2.d=21​ay​t12​+(ay​t1​)t2​.
3. Execute
t1=5.20×10−5 s,t2=1.12×10−4 st_1=5.20\times10^{-5}\ \mathrm s,\quad t_2=1.12\times10^{-4}\ \mathrm st1​=5.20×10−5 s,t2​=1.12×10−4 s qm=dE(12t12+t1t2)=2.18×103 C/kg.\frac qm=\frac{d}{E(\frac12t_1^2+t_1t_2)}=2.18\times10^3\ \mathrm{C/kg}.mq​=E(21​t12​+t1​t2​)d​=2.18×103 C/kg.
4. Evaluate
The acceleration stops outside the plates, but the vertical velocity does not vanish. Including only the displacement inside the plates would give the wrong charge-to-mass ratio.
Final answer

+2.18 × 10³ C/kg.

21.79 · Field and force from a charged rod

Positive charge Q is uniform along the x-axis from 0 to a. A positive point charge q is at x = a + r. (a) Find the field components for x > a. (b) Find the force on q. (c) Show the far-away limit when r ≫ a.

Original homework ↗ · Instructor solution key ↗, PDF page 4–5 (Camosun login).

Given
A uniform rod: length a, total charge Q > 0; observation point x > a; test charge q > 0 at x = a + r.
Find
Eₓ(x), Eᵧ(x), force on q, and the limit for r ≫ a.
Variables
a: rod length; x′: source coordinate on the rod, 0 ≤ x′ ≤ a; x: observation coordinate; r: gap from the rod end to q; Q: rod charge; q: test charge; λ = Q/a: line density; dQ: charge of one rod segment; k: Coulomb constant.

Hint

1. Identify
Each source piece is left of the observation point. All its field points along +x, so there are no y-components to add.
2. Set up
