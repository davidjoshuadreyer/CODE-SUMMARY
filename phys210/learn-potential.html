Electric potential and energy

LESSON 5 OF 13 · WEEKS 5–7 · TEXTBOOK 23.1–23.5

1. Potential is energy per charge

Potential V is measured in volts (J/C); potential energy U is in joules. A charge q has change ΔU=qΔV\Delta U=q\Delta VΔU=qΔV, and electric work is W=−ΔUW=-\Delta UW=−ΔU. With no other work, ΔK=−qΔV\Delta K=-q\Delta VΔK=−qΔV. Negative charges can gain kinetic energy while moving to higher potential.

2. Calculate V before differentiating

Taking V = 0 at infinity for localized charges, a point source has V=kQ/rV=kQ/rV=kQ/r. Potentials add as signed scalars: V=∑ikQi/riV=\sum_i kQ_i/r_iV=∑i​kQi​/ri​. For continuous charge use V=∫k dq/rV=\int k\,dq/rV=∫kdq/r. For a spherical conductor of radius R, V is kQ/RkQ/RkQ/R throughout its interior and kQ/rkQ/rkQ/r outside. Infinite lines and sheets require a finite reference potential rather than zero at infinity.

3. Connect field and equipotentials

Vb−Va=−∫abE⃗⋅dℓ⃗,E⃗=−∇V.V_b-V_a=-\int_a^b\vec E\cdot d\vec\ell,\qquad\vec E=-\nabla V.Vb​−Va​=−∫ab​E⋅dℓ,E=−∇V. In one dimension Ex=−dV/dxE_x=-dV/dxEx​=−dV/dx. Field points toward decreasing V, perpendicular to equipotential surfaces. A line with density λ gives Vb−Va=−λln⁡(rb/ra)/(2πε0)V_b-V_a=-\lambda\ln(r_b/r_a)/(2\pi\varepsilon_0)Vb​−Va​=−λln(rb​/ra​)/(2πε0​). A uniform field gives ΔV=−E⃗⋅Δr⃗\Delta V=-\vec E\cdot\Delta\vec rΔV=−E⋅Δr. Keep a reference and direction consistent.

Worked example

Given / Find

A +2.00 µC source is 0.300 m from P. Find V(P), taking zero at infinity, and U for a −1.00 nC test charge at P.

Identify: potential is a scalar. Set up: V=kQ/rV=kQ/rV=kQ/r, U=qVU=qVU=qV. Execute: V=5.99×104 VV=5.99\times10^4\,\mathrm VV=5.99×104V and U=−5.99×10−5 JU=-5.99\times10^{-5}\,\mathrm JU=−5.99×10−5J. Evaluate: negative U is consistent with attraction and the chosen zero at infinite separation.
