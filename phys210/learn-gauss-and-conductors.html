Flux, Gauss’s law, and conductors

1. Flux measures field crossing a surface

For a flat area A in a uniform field, ΦE=EAcos⁡θ\Phi_E=EA\cos\thetaΦE​=EAcosθ, where θ is between the field and the area normal, not the surface itself. Generalize to ΦE=∫E⃗⋅dA⃗\Phi_E=\int\vec E\cdot d\vec AΦE​=∫E⋅dA. For a closed surface use outward normals. Positive flux is outward; negative is inward.

2. Use Gauss only after checking symmetry

∮E⃗⋅dA⃗=Qenc/ε0.\oint\vec E\cdot d\vec A=Q_{enc}/\varepsilon_0.∮E⋅dA=Qenc​/ε0​. The law always holds, but solving for E easily requires symmetry. A centred spherical surface gives E4πr2=Qenc/ε0E4\pi r^2=Q_{enc}/\varepsilon_0E4πr2=Qenc​/ε0​. A coaxial cylinder of radius r and length ℓ around an infinite uniform line gives E2πrℓ=λℓ/ε0E2\pi r\ell=\lambda\ell/\varepsilon_0E2πrℓ=λℓ/ε0​, or E=λ/(2πε0r)E=\lambda/(2\pi\varepsilon_0r)E=λ/(2πε0​r). A pillbox through an infinite insulating sheet gives 2EA=σA/ε02EA=\sigma A/\varepsilon_02EA=σA/ε0​, or E=σ/(2ε0)E=\sigma/(2\varepsilon_0)E=σ/(2ε0​).

3. Inside matter and conductors

For a uniformly charged solid sphere of radius R and total Q, at r < R the enclosed charge is Q(r/R)3Q(r/R)^3Q(r/R)3, so E=kQr/R3E=kQr/R^3E=kQr/R3 radially (signed Q). Outside, E=kQ/r2E=kQ/r^2E=kQ/r2. In electrostatic equilibrium a conductor has E = 0 in its material, constant potential, and excess charge on surfaces. Just outside, the normal field is En=σ/ε0E_n=\sigma/\varepsilon_0En​=σ/ε0​. A charge q in an empty internal cavity induces −q on its inner conductor surface.

Worked example

Given / Find

A sphere encloses +3.00 nC. Find its outward flux and the field at r = 0.200 m if the distribution is spherically symmetric and entirely inside that radius.

Identify: flux depends on enclosed charge. Set up: ΦE=Q/ε0\Phi_E=Q/\varepsilon_0ΦE​=Q/ε0​ and E=ΦE/(4πr2)E=\Phi_E/(4\pi r^2)E=ΦE​/(4πr2). Execute: ΦE=339 N m2/C\Phi_E=339\,\mathrm{N\,m^2/C}ΦE​=339Nm2/C and E=674 N/CE=674\,\mathrm{N/C}E=674N/C outward. Evaluate: doubling radius leaves flux fixed but quarters E.

Try it yourself

Can zero enclosed charge prove zero field everywhere on the surface?

Show explanation

No. It proves zero net flux. External charges can produce nonzero fields whose inward and outward fluxes cancel.
