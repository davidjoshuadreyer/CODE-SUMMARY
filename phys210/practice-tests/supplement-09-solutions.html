QUESTION 1 · Tutorial 5 · 34

Energy of a four-charge rectangle

Exact question and original solution screenshots ↗

  1. Count all six distinct charge pairs

    There are two vertical pairs, two horizontal pairs and two diagonal pairs; count each once. Each diagonal has length D = √(a²+b²). U=kq2[2−6a+−2+6b+3−4a2+b2]U=kq^2\left[\frac{2-6}{a}+\frac{-2+6}{b}+\frac{3-4}{\sqrt{a^2+b^2}}\right]U=kq2[a2−6​+b−2+6​+a2+b2​3−4​]

  2. Evaluate the sum

    Vertical terms are attractive overall; horizontal terms are repulsive overall; diagonal terms are attractive overall. U=(8.988×109)(6.00×10−6)2[−40.200+40.400−10.447214]=−3.959 JU=(8.988\times10^9)(6.00\times10^{-6})^2\left[-\frac4{0.200}+\frac4{0.400}-\frac1{0.447214}\right]=-3.959\ \mathrm JU=(8.988×109)(6.00×10−6)2[−0.2004​+0.4004​−0.4472141​]=−3.959 J

  3. Interpret the sign

    The external agent does negative quasistatic work; 3.96 J is released during assembly.
Wext=ΔU=−3.96 JW_{\mathrm{ext}}=\Delta U=-3.96\ \mathrm JWext​=ΔU=−3.96 J

The formula contains exactly 4·3/2 = 6 pair interactions. A negative result represents released energy, not a negative energy requirement magnitude.
