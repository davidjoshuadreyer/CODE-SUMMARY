QUESTION 1 · Tutorial 6 · 23

Charge sharing after switching

Exact question and original solution screenshots ↗

  1. Charge the first capacitor

    Initially only C₁ is connected across the battery. Q1i=C1V0=(6.00)(20.0)=120 μCQ_{1i}=C_1V_0=(6.00)(20.0)=120\ \mu\mathrm CQ1i​=C1​V0​=(6.00)(20.0)=120 μC

  2. Conserve charge after disconnecting the battery

    Both capacitors share a final voltage. Total positive-plate charge remains 120 μC. Vf=Q1iC1+C2=1209.00=13.33 VV_f=\frac{Q_{1i}}{C_1+C_2}=\frac{120}{9.00}=13.33\ \mathrm VVf​=C1​+C2​Q1i​​=9.00120​=13.33 V

  3. Allocate the final charge

    Each final charge is its capacitance times the common voltage. Q1f=C1Vf=80.0 μC,Q2f=C2Vf=40.0 μCQ_{1f}=C_1V_f=80.0\ \mu\mathrm C,\qquad Q_{2f}=C_2V_f=40.0\ \mu\mathrm CQ1f​=C1​Vf​=80.0 μC,Q2f​=C2​Vf​=40.0 μC
Q1i=120 μC;Q1f=80.0 μC;Q2f=40.0 μCQ_{1i}=120\ \mu\mathrm C;\quad Q_{1f}=80.0\ \mu\mathrm C;\quad Q_{2f}=40.0\ \mu\mathrm CQ1i​=120 μC;Q1f​=80.0 μC;Q2f​=40.0 μC

Final positive-plate charges sum to 120 μC; each negative plate has the opposite charge. Electrostatic energy is not conserved during charge redistribution.
