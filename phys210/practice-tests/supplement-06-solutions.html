QUESTION 3 · Homework 5 · 23.19

Potentials at two points

Exact question and original solution screenshots ↗

  1. Identify

    Potential is a signed scalar sum; work uses start minus finish.

  2. Set up

    V=k(q1/r1+q2/r2); WB→A=qt(VB−VA)V=k(q_1/r_1+q_2/r_2);\ W_{B\to A}=q_t(V_B-V_A)V=k(q1​/r1​+q2​/r2​); WB→A​=qt​(VB​−VA​).

  3. Execute

    VA=−737 V; VB=−704 V; W=(2.50×10−9)(32.96)=8.24×10−8 JV_A=-737\ \mathrm V;\ V_B=-704\ \mathrm V;\ W=(2.50\times10^{-9})(32.96)=8.24\times10^{-8}\ \mathrm JVA​=−737 V; VB​=−704 V; W=(2.50×10−9)(32.96)=8.24×10−8 J.

    The positive test charge moves to lower potential, so the field does positive work.

QUESTION 4 · Homework 5 · 23.27

Electron on a charged ring axis

Exact question and original solution screenshots ↗

  1. Identify

    The attractive force restores the electron toward the ring plane.

  2. Set up

    V(x)=kQ/R2+x2; 12mev2=e[V(0)−V(x0)]V(x)=kQ/\sqrt{R^2+x^2};\ \tfrac12m_ev^2=e[V(0)-V(x_0)]V(x)=kQ/R2+x2​; 21​me​v2=e[V(0)−V(x0​)].

  4. Evaluate

    It oscillates between ±0.300 m, fastest at the centre. This finite-amplitude motion is not simple harmonic because force is not proportional to x.
