Electric fields, motion, and dipoles

LESSON 2 OF 13 · WEEKS 2–3 · TEXTBOOK 21.4–21.7

1. Separate the source from the test charge

The field is force per unit positive test charge: E⃗=F⃗/q\vec E=\vec F/qE=F/q. For source charge QQQ, E=k∣Q∣/r2E=k|Q|/r^2E=k∣Q∣/r2 (N/C), outward from positive Q and inward toward negative Q. A test charge q then feels F⃗=qE⃗\vec F=q\vec EF=qE. Negative q reverses the force direction. Superposition means adding field vectors before multiplying by q.

2. Motion and equilibrium

In a uniform field, a⃗=qE⃗/m\vec a=q\vec E/ma=qE/m, where m is particle mass. Split motion into axes; the axis with no force has constant velocity. If a charged object hangs at rest, draw tension, weight, and electric force and apply ∑Fx=∑Fy=0\sum F_x=\sum F_y=0∑Fx​=∑Fy​=0. Field lines indicate the direction of force on positive charge; closer spacing means a stronger field. Lines never cross.

3. Dipoles

A dipole contains charges +q+q+q and −q-q−q separated by vector d⃗\vec dd from negative to positive. Its dipole moment is p⃗=qd⃗\vec p=q\vec dp​=qd (C m). In uniform E the net force is zero, but the torque is τ⃗=p⃗×E⃗\vec\tau=\vec p\times\vec Eτ=p​×E, magnitude pEsin⁡θpE\sin\thetapEsinθ. Its potential energy is U=−pEcos⁡θU=-pE\cos\thetaU=−pEcosθ. Here θ is between p and E. Parallel alignment is stable. In nonuniform fields a dipole may also experience net force.

Worked example

Given / Find

A proton is in a uniform field of 200 N/C to the right. Find its acceleration; e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and mₚ = 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

Identify: the proton is positive, so force is rightward. Set up: a=eE/mpa=eE/m_pa=eE/mp​. Execute: a=1.92×1010 m/s2a=1.92\times10^{10}\,\mathrm{m/s^2}a=1.92×1010m/s2 to the right. Evaluate: using an electron reverses direction and increases acceleration magnitude because its mass is smaller.
