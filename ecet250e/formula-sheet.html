Nodal & mesh analysis

Nodal: select ground, label voltages, and write KCL. A voltage source between unknown nodes creates a supernode: add its voltage constraint. Mesh (planar circuits): choose clockwise mesh currents; Rkk is total mesh resistance and Rkj is shared resistance. A shared current source creates a supermesh: add its current constraint. Keep dependent-source control equations.

CHAPTER 04

Superposition & source conversion

v=∑kvk,i=∑kikv=\sum_kv_k,\qquad i=\sum_ki_kv=k∑​vk​,i=k∑​ik​Vs=IsR,Is=VsRV_s=I_sR,\qquad I_s=\frac{V_s}{R}Vs​=Is​R,Is​=RVs​​

Superposition: activate one independent source at a time. Replace an ideal independent voltage source by a short and an ideal independent current source by an open. Dependent sources stay active. Sum voltages/currents, then calculate power; power does not superpose. Source conversion pairs a series voltage source + R with a parallel current source + R; match terminal polarity and current arrow.

Remove the load to find the equivalent seen at its terminals. For resistance, deactivate independent sources and retain dependent sources; use a test source if needed, with test current entering the positive terminal. Voc/Isc also works when the ratio is defined. Maximum-power formulas assume a positive resistive RTh and an adjustable resistive load.

Ideal constant C; passive sign convention. Capacitor voltage is continuous unless current contains an impulse: vC(0⁺) = vC(0⁻). At DC steady state, an ideal capacitor is an open circuit. For two series capacitors, C = C₁C₂/(C₁+C₂).

CHAPTER 06

Inductors

vL=LdiLdt,wL=12LiL2v_L=L\frac{di_L}{dt},\quad w_L=\frac12Li_L^2vL​=LdtdiL​​,wL​=21​LiL2​iL(t)=iL(t0)+1L∫t0tvL(τ) dτi_L(t)=i_L(t_0)+\frac1L\int_{t_0}^{t}v_L(\tau)\,d\tauiL​(t)=iL​(t0​)+L1​∫t0​t​vL​(τ)dτLseries=∑kLk,1L∥=∑k1LkL_{\mathrm{series}}=\sum_kL_k,\qquad \frac1{L_{\parallel}}=\sum_k\frac1{L_k}Lseries​=k∑​Lk​,L∥​1​=k∑​Lk​1​

Ideal constant L; passive sign convention. Inductor current is continuous unless voltage contains an impulse: iL(0⁺) = iL(0⁻). At DC steady state, an ideal inductor is a short circuit. Equivalent-inductance rules assume no mutual coupling.

Units: current A · charge C · voltage V · resistance Ω · conductance S · power W · energy J · capacitance F · inductance H. Prefixes: G 10⁹ · M 10⁶ · k 10³ · m 10⁻³ · μ 10⁻⁶ · n 10⁻⁹ · p 10⁻¹². Convert to consistent units before calculating.
