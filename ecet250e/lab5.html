Preparation

1 · Build once and label the nodes

Keep the original circuit on the left and the equivalent circuit on the right. Use one supply channel and move its two leads between circuits with its output OFF. Do not join the two circuits.

  1. Gather a breadboard, DC supply, DMM, insulated jumpers, 220 Ω, 330 Ω, 680 Ω, 1 kΩ, an equivalent resistance close to 812 Ω, and loads of 100, 470, 820, 1200, 1800, 2700 and 3300 Ω. An 820 Ω resistor is a practical equivalent-resistance option too; record it separately from the 820 Ω load.
  2. With parts disconnected, check resistor values in Ω mode and enter actual values below. Build the left circuit first. Set the supply output OFF; use a suitable current limit (about 50 mA is sufficient for this circuit).
  3. Use the diagram’s row groups only if your board has standard five-hole strips. In each numbered row, a–e connect together and f–j connect together; the centre gap separates them. Different numbered rows do not connect. This layout does not use power rails.

The drawing shows electrical endpoints. Use insulated jumpers if a resistor cannot reach both holes. Do not leave exposed leads touching intermediate nodes. Colours are paired with node labels.

2 · Measure the original circuit

Leave RL = 1 kΩ connected. Take both voltage readings first, then both current readings, to minimize DMM lead changes.

  1. Voltage: black lead in COM, red lead in VΩ, DMM on DC volts. Connect red to A (e15), black to B (e20), across RL. Set supply to 12 V, enable output, record VL. Set it to 15 V and record VL again. Record actual supply voltages if checked.
  2. Current: turn output OFF. Move RL’s top lead from d15 to free row d17, leaving its bottom at d20. Put the red lead in the DMM’s fused mA input and select DC mA. Connect red to e15 and black to e17. The meter now bridges the break in series with RL.
  3. Set supply to 12 V and enable output; record IL in mA. Set 15 V and record IL. Switch output OFF. Restore RL to d15–d20, return the red lead to VΩ and select DC volts.

A current meter must bridge an opened wire. Do not connect a meter in mA mode directly across A–B or the supply. Enter current in mA, not A.

Table B · Vth

3 · Measure open-circuit voltage

  1. With output OFF, remove RL entirely from the original circuit. Leave R1, R2 and R3 in place.
  2. Connect the DMM in DC volts across A–B: red e15, black e20. Set supply to 12 V, enable output and record Vth. Repeat at 15 V.
  3. Switch output OFF. Leave RL removed for the resistance measurement.

Expected Vth: 7.200 V at 12 V and 9.000 V at 15 V. Removing RL is essential. A reading near the loaded voltage means the load may still be connected.

Table B · Rth

4 · Measure resistance with the supply disconnected

  1. Supply OFF, RL removed. Physically disconnect both supply leads from the original network.
  2. Add a jumper e5 → b20, joining circuit supply node S to B. This replaces the removed ideal voltage source with a short inside the unpowered network.
  3. Use DMM Ω mode, red in VΩ. Measure between e15 (A) and e20 (B). Record Rth below. One measurement applies to both source cases because the resistors have not changed.
  4. Remove the shorting jumper before reconnecting any supply. Restore RL = 1 kΩ. Keep this original circuit intact for photos and comparisons.

Short only the disconnected network’s S–B nodes. Never short the supply terminals. Never measure resistance in an energized circuit.

5 · Test the equivalent circuit

Original 12 V case → equivalent supply 7.2 V.
Original 15 V case → equivalent supply 9.0 V.
The equivalent contains only the source, Rth and RL. Do not carry the original 12/15 V settings across.

  1. With output OFF, wire the right-half circuit shown in Step 1. Use the measured equivalent resistor value recorded there and RL = 1 kΩ. Move supply leads to i5 (+) and i20 (−).
  2. For voltage, red VΩ lead at h10 (A′), black COM at h20 (B′). Set 7.2 V and record VL, then set 9.0 V and record VL. Record actual source voltage in each case.
  3. Output OFF. For current, move RL’s top from g10 to g14. Select DC mA with the red lead in the fused mA input. Connect red j10 and black j14 to bridge the break. Record IL at 7.2 V and then 9.0 V.
  4. Output OFF. Restore RL to g10–g20. Return red lead to VΩ and DC volts. Leave the voltmeter across A′–B′ for the entire sweep.

6 · Sweep the loads with one voltage hookup

Keep the equivalent circuit and voltmeter connected. For each load, take both supply cases before changing the resistor. This needs only seven load installations.

  1. Output OFF. Replace the load at g10–g20 with the first staged resistor. Confirm its actual resistance is entered below.
  2. Set equivalent supply to 7.2 V, enable output, and enter VL in the 12 V original case column.
  3. Set equivalent supply to 9.0 V and enter VL in the 15 V original case column.
  4. Output OFF before changing the load. Repeat down the table. Power calculates from your measured voltage and actual load resistance.

PL (mW) = 1000 × VL² (V²) ÷ RL (Ω)

Finish at the bench

7 · Check, photograph and save

  1. Review any blank fields and confirm current units are mA. Check that the original and equivalent readings are close, and that power peaks near the equivalent resistance.
  2. Take clear photos of both circuits with resistor and jumper connections visible. Include meter and supply readings where useful.
  3. Save a readings backup and export the Excel results. Copy the values into Tables A–E of the report. The measured Rth is used in both Table B columns. Table C uses the highest measured power from each sweep; record the corresponding actual load.
  4. Make the two required Excel graphs and finish the discussion using measured evidence. Switch supply OFF before packing up.
