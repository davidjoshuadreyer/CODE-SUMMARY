Current, voltage, and power

Define reference directions before using a formula.

Given: A resistor R = 6.00 Ω is connected across a 12.0 V DC source.

Find: The resistor current and the power it absorbs.

Define the variables

V: voltage across the resistor (V); I: current through it (A); R: resistance (Ω); P: absorbed power (W). Current enters the resistor’s positive voltage terminal.

Worked example

  1. Use Ohm’s law

    I=VR=12.06.00=2.00 AI=\frac{V}{R}=\frac{12.0}{6.00}=2.00\ \mathrm AI=RV​=6.0012.0​=2.00 A

  2. Use the passive sign convention

    When current enters the terminal marked +, P = VI is the power absorbed by that element.

  3. Calculate power

    P=VI=(12.0)(2.00)=24.0 WP=VI=(12.0)(2.00)=24.0\ \mathrm WP=VI=(12.0)(2.00)=24.0 W

  4. Interpret it

    The resistor converts electrical energy into heat at 24.0 joules per second. The ideal source supplies that power.
Check your answer

P = I²R = (2.00)²(6.00) = 24.0 W gives the same answer.

Try it yourself

What happens to current and power if the resistance doubles while voltage stays fixed?

Show the worked answer

I = 12.0/12.0 = 1.00 A and P = 12.0 W. Both halve.
