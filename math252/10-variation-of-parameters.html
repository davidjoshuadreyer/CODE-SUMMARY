Variation of parameters

Use a general method when a simple trial function will not work.

Before you start: Integration, Wronskians, and homogeneous solutions.

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Let the constants become functions

Normalize to y′′+Py′+Qy=gy''+Py'+Qy=gy′′+Py′+Qy=g, and find independent homogeneous solutions y1,y2y_1,y_2y1​,y2​. Seek yp=u1y1+u2y2y_p=u_1y_1+u_2y_2yp​=u1​y1​+u2​y2​. Impose u1′y1+u2′y2=0u_1'y_1+u_2'y_2=0u1′​y1​+u2′​y2​=0 to simplify the derivatives.

Solve for the new derivatives

Using W=y1y2′−y1′y2W=y_1y_2'-y_1'y_2W=y1​y2′​−y1′​y2​, u1′=−y2gW,u2′=y1gW.u_1'=-\frac{y_2g}{W},\qquad u_2'=\frac{y_1g}{W}.u1′​=−Wy2​g​,u2′​=Wy1​g​. Integrate these and substitute into ypy_pyp​. Constants of integration in the uuu's can be set to zero because their contributions are already in ycy_cyc​.

Your method checklist

  1. Divide by the leading coefficient before identifying g.
  2. Find y₁, y₂, and the Wronskian.
  3. Integrate the two parameter derivatives.
  4. Form y_p and add y_c; keep the domain of all functions.

A forcing term outside the trial table

Solve y′′+y=sec⁡xy''+y=\sec xy′′+y=secx on −π/2<x<π/2-\pi/2<x<\pi/2−π/2<x<π/2.

    Find the homogeneous pair

    Take y1=cos⁡xy_1=\cos xy1​=cosx, y2=sin⁡xy_2=\sin xy2​=sinx, giving W=cos⁡2x+sin⁡2x=1W=\cos^2x+\sin^2x=1W=cos2x+sin2x=1.
