Nonhomogeneous systems

Combine the system’s natural modes with a response to external forcing.

Before you start: Linear systems, scalar integrating factors, and matrix inverses.

UnderstandMethodExample 1Your turnAvoid this mistake

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Add a particular vector solution

For X′=AX+f(t),\mathbf X'=A\mathbf X+\mathbf f(t),X′=AX+f(t), the general solution is X=Xc+Xp\mathbf X=\mathbf X_c+\mathbf X_pX=Xc​+Xp​. A simple constant or exponential forcing may allow a vector trial, but resonance can require a factor ttt.

Variation of parameters for systems

Let Φ(t)\Phi(t)Φ(t) be a fundamental matrix whose columns are independent homogeneous solutions. Setting X=Φu\mathbf X=\Phi\mathbf uX=Φu gives Φu′=f\Phi\mathbf u'=\mathbf fΦu′=f. Therefore X=Φ(t)[c+∫Φ(t)−1f(t) dt].\mathbf X=\Phi(t)\left[\mathbf c+\int\Phi(t)^{-1}\mathbf f(t)\,dt\right].X=Φ(t)[c+∫Φ(t)−1f(t)dt]. Matrix order matters; multiply Φ−1\Phi^{-1}Φ−1 by f\mathbf ff, not the other way around.

Use the structure when possible

If one equation does not depend on the other unknown, solve it first, then substitute into the remaining equation. This is often simpler than computing a fundamental matrix. A scalar second-order equation can also be rewritten as a system using X1=yX_1=yX1​=y, X2=y′X_2=y'X2​=y′.

Your method checklist

  1. Find the homogeneous solution or identify a triangular structure.
  2. Choose a simple particular trial or use variation of parameters.
  3. Add the two parts and apply the initial vector.
  4. Check both equations, including their forcing terms.
