Reading a differential equation

Understand what you are solving for, what a derivative means, and what an initial condition adds.

Before you start: Basic derivatives and substitution.

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

The unknown is a function

An algebra equation asks for a number. A differential equation asks for a function whose derivatives satisfy a relationship. In y′=2xy'=2xy′=2x, the unknown is y(x)y(x)y(x). The prime is shorthand for dy/dxdy/dxdy/dx, the rate at which yyy changes with xxx.

Order and linearity

The order is the highest derivative present: y′′+y=0y''+y=0y′′+y=0 is second order. A linear equation contains the unknown function and its derivatives only to the first power, with coefficients depending only on the independent variable. Thus xy′+y=x2xy'+y=x^2xy′+y=x2 is linear, but y′=y2y'=y^2y′=y2 and yy′′=1yy''=1yy′′=1 are nonlinear.

A family versus one member

Integrating y′=2xy'=2xy′=2x gives the family y=x2+Cy=x^2+Cy=x2+C. An initial condition, such as y(0)=3y(0)=3y(0)=3, selects a member. A solution must be differentiable enough and satisfy the equation on an interval, not just at one point.

When an IVP has one local solution

For y′=f(x,y)y'=f(x,y)y′=f(x,y), continuity of fff and ∂f/∂y\partial f/\partial y∂f/∂y in a rectangle around the initial point is a sufficient condition for one local solution. If these conditions fail, the test is inconclusive; it does not automatically mean no solution exists.

Your method checklist

  1. Identify the independent variable, unknown function, and highest derivative.
  2. Differentiate a proposed solution before substituting it into the equation.
  3. Use every initial condition and state any interval restrictions.

Your turn

Show the step-by-step solution
  1. 1

    Integrate

    y=3x2+C.y=3x^2+C.y=3x2+C. One integration introduces one arbitrary constant.

  2. 2

Avoid this mistake

A solution must satisfy both the equation and the initial condition. Checking only one is not enough.
