First-order linear equations

Before you start: The product rule and integration.

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Normalize first

The standard form is y′+P(x)y=Q(x).y'+P(x)y=Q(x).y′+P(x)y=Q(x). Divide by the coefficient of y′y'y′ before identifying PPP. Work on an interval where that coefficient is nonzero.

Why the integrating factor works

Choose μ(x)=e∫P(x) dx.\mu(x)=e^{\int P(x)\,dx}.μ(x)=e∫P(x)dx. Because μ′=Pμ\mu'=P\muμ′=Pμ, multiplying the equation by μ\muμ makes μy′+μPy=(μy)′\mu y'+\mu Py=(\mu y)'μy′+μPy=(μy)′. Hence y=1μ(∫μQ dx+C).y=\frac{1}{\mu}\left(\int\mu Q\,dx+C\right).y=μ1​(∫μQdx+C). You may choose one convenient antiderivative in the exponent; an extra constant factor is unnecessary.

Your method checklist

  1. Divide into standard form.
  2. Find the integrating factor.
  3. Multiply every term and identify the product derivative.
  4. Integrate, divide by the integrating factor, and apply the initial condition.
