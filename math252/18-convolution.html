MATH 252 / LESSON 18 / SECTIONS 7.4

Convolution and transform operations

Recognize products of transforms and handle time factors without starting a new integral from scratch.

Before you start: Laplace transforms, derivatives, and definite integration.

UnderstandMethodExample 1Your turnAvoid this mistake

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

A product in s becomes a convolution

Define (f∗g)(t)=∫0tf(τ)g(t−τ) dτ.(f*g)(t)=\int_0^t f(\tau)g(t-\tau)\,d\tau.(f∗g)(t)=∫0t​f(τ)g(t−τ)dτ. Then L{f∗g}=F(s)G(s)\mathcal L\{f*g\}=F(s)G(s)L{f∗g}=F(s)G(s). The integration variable τ\tauτ is a placeholder; ttt stays fixed during the integral.

Time multiplication becomes differentiation

L{tf(t)}=−F′(s),L{tnf(t)}=(−1)nF(n)(s).\mathcal L\{t f(t)\}=-F'(s),\qquad \mathcal L\{t^n f(t)\}=(-1)^nF^{(n)}(s).L{tf(t)}=−F′(s),L{tnf(t)}=(−1)nF(n)(s). For example, differentiating 1/(s−a)1/(s-a)1/(s−a) gives L{teat}=1/(s−a)2\mathcal L\{te^{at}\}=1/(s-a)^2L{teat}=1/(s−a)2.

Integration in time

L{∫0tf(τ) dτ}=F(s)s.\mathcal L\left\{\int_0^t f(\tau)\,d\tau\right\}=\frac{F(s)}s.L{∫0t​f(τ)dτ}=sF(s)​. This is convolution with the constant function 1.

Optional: a repeating input

For a piecewise continuous function repeating with period TTT, L{f}=∫0Te−stf(t) dt1−e−sT,s>0.\mathcal L\{f\}=\frac{\int_0^T e^{-st}f(t)\,dt}{1-e^{-sT}},\qquad s>0.L{f}=1−e−sT∫0T​e−stf(t)dt​,s>0. The instructor’s periodic-function supplement is marked not tested and intended for electronics students. Learn the main operations above first.

Your method checklist

  1. Factor the transform into recognizable pieces.
  2. Identify the two inverse functions.
  3. Set up the convolution with limits 0 and t.
  4. Integrate, simplify, and compare with partial fractions when possible.

Avoid this mistake

In a convolution, g must be evaluated at t−τ. Multiplying f(t)g(t) is a different operation.
