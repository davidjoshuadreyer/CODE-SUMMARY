Substitutions: homogeneous and Bernoulli

Replace a difficult expression with a new variable so a familiar method becomes available.

Before you start: Separation, integrating factors, and the chain rule.

UnderstandMethodExample 1Example 2Your turnAvoid this mistake

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

A ratio suggests y=vx

If y′=F(y/x)y'=F(y/x)y′=F(y/x), set v=y/xv=y/xv=y/x, or y=xvy=xvy=xv. The product rule gives y′=v+xv′.y'=v+xv'.y′=v+xv′. Substitute both expressions. The resulting equation in vvv and xxx is often separable. Work away from x=0x=0x=0. Here “homogeneous” refers to dependence on the ratio, not to a zero forcing term.

A power suggests Bernoulli

For y′+P(x)y=Q(x)yn,n≠0,1,y'+P(x)y=Q(x)y^n,\qquad n\ne0,1,y′+P(x)y=Q(x)yn,n=0,1, set v=y1−nv=y^{1-n}v=y1−n. On a suitable nonzero branch, dividing by yny^nyn and applying the chain rule gives v′+(1−n)Pv=(1−n)Q.v'+(1-n)Pv=(1-n)Q.v′+(1−n)Pv=(1−n)Q. This is linear in vvv. Check lost zero solutions in the original equation.

A combined expression can be the variable

For y′=f(ax+by+c)y'=f(ax+by+c)y′=f(ax+by+c), try u=ax+by+cu=ax+by+cu=ax+by+c. Then u′=a+by′u'=a+b y'u′=a+by′, giving u′=a+bf(u)u'=a+b f(u)u′=a+bf(u), which is separable when applicable. Always translate the initial condition into the new variable.

Your method checklist

  1. Recognize the pattern and define the new variable.
  2. Differentiate the substitution carefully.
  3. Solve the transformed equation.
  4. Substitute back and check the original equation.

Solve y′+y=y2y'+y=y^2y′+y=y2, y(0)=1/2y(0)=1/2y(0)=1/2.

    Here n=2n=2n=2, so v=y−1v=y^{-1}v=y−1. Dividing by y2y^2y2 gives −v′+v=1-v'+v=1−v′+v=1, or v′−v=−1v'-v=-1v′−v=−1.
