Growth, cooling, and mixing

Translate a short word problem into a rate equation with consistent units.

Before you start: Separation, first-order linear equations, and logarithms.

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Growth or decay

“Rate proportional to the amount” means A′=kAA'=kAA′=kA, so A=A0ektA=A_0e^{kt}A=A0​ekt. Positive kkk means growth and negative kkk means decay. Use a measurement to find kkk; do not guess it from a percentage without accounting for elapsed time.

Cooling toward the surroundings

For constant ambient temperature TaT_aTa​, Newton’s model is T′=−k(T−Ta),k>0.T'=-k(T-T_a),\qquad k>0.T′=−k(T−Ta​),k>0. Thus T−Ta=(T0−Ta)e−ktT-T_a=(T_0-T_a)e^{-kt}T−Ta​=(T0​−Ta​)e−kt. The decaying quantity is the temperature difference, not the temperature itself.

Mixing is a balance

Let mmm be the mass of dissolved salt. Then m′=mass rate in−mass rate out.m'=\text{mass rate in}-\text{mass rate out}.m′=mass rate in−mass rate out. With flow in litres per minute and concentration in grams per litre, each product is grams per minute. The outgoing concentration is m(t)/V(t)m(t)/V(t)m(t)/V(t). If the flows differ, use V(t)=V0+(qin−qout)tV(t)=V_0+(q_{in}-q_{out})tV(t)=V0​+(qin​−qout​)t while the tank model remains valid.

Your method checklist

  1. Define the quantity, its units, and the time unit.
  2. Translate each rate separately before simplifying.
  3. Solve the IVP, then answer the question in words and units.
  4. Check the initial value and long-term behaviour.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A well-mixed tank

A tank holds 100 L of fresh water. Brine at 2 g/L enters at 5 L/min; mixed liquid leaves at 5 L/min. Find the salt mass after t minutes.

  1. 1

    Write the two rates

    Input is 2⋅5=102\cdot5=102⋅5=10 g/min. Output is (m/100)⋅5=m/20(m/100)\cdot5=m/20(m/100)⋅5=m/20 g/min. Equal flows keep the volume at 100 L.

  2. 2

    Build and solve the equation

    m′+120m=10,m(0)=0.m'+\frac1{20}m=10,\qquad m(0)=0.m′+201​m=10,m(0)=0. A constant particular solution is 200, giving m=200+Ce−t/20m=200+Ce^{-t/20}m=200+Ce−t/20.

  3. 3

    Apply the initial mass

    0=200+C0=200+C0=200+C, so C=−200C=-200C=−200.

RESULTm(t)=200(1−e−t/20) g,t≥0m(t)=200(1-e^{-t/20})\ \text{g},\qquad t\ge0m(t)=200(1−e−t/20) g,t≥0

Check the answer

At long times the concentration tends to 200/100=2200/100=2200/100=2 g/L, the incoming concentration.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A doubling time

A population starts at 200 and doubles in 3 hours. Find its exponential model.

  1. 1

    Start with the initial amount

    A(t)=200ektA(t)=200e^{kt}A(t)=200ekt.

  2. 2

    Use the second measurement

    400=200e3k⇒k=ln⁡23.400=200e^{3k}\quad\Rightarrow\quad k=\frac{\ln2}{3}.400=200e3k⇒k=3ln2​.

  3. 3

    Express the model

    Each three-hour period contributes one factor of 2.

RESULTA(t)=200e(ln⁡2)t/3=200⋅2t/3A(t)=200e^{(\ln2)t/3}=200\cdot2^{t/3}A(t)=200e(ln2)t/3=200⋅2t/3

Check the answer

At 6 hours, A=800A=800A=800, which is two doublings.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Your turn

An object cools from 80°C to 50°C in 10 minutes in a 20°C room. When does it reach 35°C?

Try the method on paper first. The full solution is available whenever you need it.

Show the step-by-step solution
  1. 1

    Model the difference

    T=20+60e−kt.T=20+60e^{-kt}.T=20+60e−kt.

  2. 2

    Find k

    50=20+60e−10k50=20+60e^{-10k}50=20+60e−10k gives e−10k=1/2e^{-10k}=1/2e−10k=1/2, so k=ln⁡2/10k=\ln2/10k=ln2/10.

  3. 3

    Solve for the requested temperature

    35=20+60e−kt35=20+60e^{-kt}35=20+60e−kt gives e−kt=1/4e^{-kt}=1/4e−kt=1/4. Hence t=ln⁡4/k=20t=\ln4/k=20t=ln4/k=20.

RESULTt=20 minutest=20\ \text{minutes}t=20 minutes

Check the answer

The excess temperature halves every 10 minutes: 60, then 30, then 15 degrees.

Avoid this mistake

In a tank problem, mass and concentration are different quantities. Use mass divided by volume for the outgoing concentration.
