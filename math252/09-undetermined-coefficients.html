MATH 252 / LESSON 09 / SECTIONS 4.4

Undetermined coefficients and resonance

Choose a trial particular solution and determine its coefficients by substitution.

Before you start: Characteristic roots and matching coefficients.

UnderstandMethodExample 1Example 2Your turnAvoid this mistake

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Choose this method for familiar forcing

For a constant-coefficient linear equation, this method works for sums of polynomial, exponential, sine, and cosine terms and their products. A polynomial of degree nnn needs a trial polynomial with every power down to the constant term.

Match the family, not just the visible term

For eaxe^{ax}eax, try AeaxAe^{ax}Aeax. For sin⁡bx\sin bxsinbx or cos⁡bx\cos bxcosbx, include both: Acos⁡bx+Bsin⁡bxA\cos bx+B\sin bxAcosbx+Bsinbx. For a polynomial times eaxe^{ax}eax, multiply a full trial polynomial of the same degree by eaxe^{ax}eax.

Fix overlap with the complementary solution

If the trial overlaps ycy_cyc​, multiply the whole trial by enough powers of xxx to remove the overlap. A simple characteristic root needs one factor xxx; a double root needs x2x^2x2. This is the resonance adjustment.

Your method checklist

  1. Find the complementary solution first.
  2. Choose a trial matching the forcing and correct any overlap.
  3. Differentiate and substitute, then solve for the trial coefficients.
  4. Add the complementary solution before applying initial conditions.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A forcing term with no overlap

Solve y′′−y=e2xy''-y=e^{2x}y′′−y=e2x in general form.

  1. 1

    Find the complementary solution

    Roots ±1\pm1±1 give yc=C1ex+C2e−xy_c=C_1e^x+C_2e^{-x}yc​=C1​ex+C2​e−x.

  2. 2

    Choose and substitute the trial

    Since 2 is not a root, try yp=Ae2xy_p=Ae^{2x}yp​=Ae2x. Then yp′′−yp=(4A−A)e2x=3Ae2x.y_p''-y_p=(4A-A)e^{2x}=3Ae^{2x}.yp′′​−yp​=(4A−A)e2x=3Ae2x.

  3. 3

    Match the coefficient

    3A=13A=13A=1, so A=1/3A=1/3A=1/3.

RESULTy=C1ex+C2e−x+13e2xy=C_1e^x+C_2e^{-x}+\frac13e^{2x}y=C1​ex+C2​e−x+31​e2x

Check the answer

The complementary terms contribute zero and the last term contributes exactly e2xe^{2x}e2x.

WORKED EXAMPLE

A resonant forcing term

Find a particular solution of y′′−y=exy''-y=e^xy′′−y=ex.

  1. 1

    Notice the overlap

    exe^xex is already in ycy_cyc​. The trial AexAe^xAex would contribute zero, so use AxexAxe^xAxex.

  2. 2

    Differentiate

    yp′=Aex(x+1),yp′′=Aex(x+2).y_p'=Ae^x(x+1),\qquad y_p''=Ae^x(x+2).yp′​=Aex(x+1),yp′′​=Aex(x+2).

  3. 3

    Substitute

    yp′′−yp=2Aexy_p''-y_p=2Ae^xyp′′​−yp​=2Aex, so A=1/2A=1/2A=1/2.

RESULTyp=12xexy_p=\frac12xe^xyp​=21​xex

Check the answer

The factor xxx makes the trial independent of the complementary solution.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Your turn

Find the general solution of y′′+y=2y''+y=2y′′+y=2.

Try the method on paper first. The full solution is available whenever you need it.

Show the step-by-step solution
  1. 1

    Solve the homogeneous equation

    Roots ±i\pm i±i give yc=C1cos⁡x+C2sin⁡xy_c=C_1\cos x+C_2\sin xyc​=C1​cosx+C2​sinx.

  2. 2

    Try a constant

    Set yp=Ay_p=Ayp​=A. Its second derivative vanishes, so A=2A=2A=2.

RESULTy=C1cos⁡x+C2sin⁡x+2y=C_1\cos x+C_2\sin x+2y=C1​cosx+C2​sinx+2

Check the answer

The sine and cosine terms cancel with their second derivatives, leaving 2.

Avoid this mistake

Initial conditions apply to y=y_c+y_p, not to y_c alone.
