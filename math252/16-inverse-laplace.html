Inverse transforms and initial-value problems

Use algebra and partial fractions to recover a time-domain solution.

Before you start: Transform pairs, partial fractions, and completing the square.

UnderstandMethodExample 1Your turnAvoid this mistake

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Undo the transform by matching pairs

Before taking an inverse, simplify the rational expression. Use polynomial division if needed, then partial fractions. For a repeated factor (s−a)m(s-a)^m(s−a)m, include every power from 1 through mmm. An irreducible quadratic needs a linear numerator.

Initial conditions enter automatically

Writing Y=L{y}Y=\mathcal L\{y\}Y=L{y}, L{y′}=sY−y(0),L{y′′}=s2Y−sy(0)−y′(0).\mathcal L\{y'\}=sY-y(0),\qquad \mathcal L\{y''\}=s^2Y-sy(0)-y'(0).L{y′}=sY−y(0),L{y′′}=s2Y−sy(0)−y′(0). Transform the entire equation, solve for YYY, and then invert term by term.

Prepare a quadratic for the table

Complete the square and rewrite the numerator in terms of the shifted variable. For example, s2−4s+13=(s−2)2+9s^2-4s+13=(s-2)^2+9s2−4s+13=(s−2)2+9. A numerator s−2s-2s−2 matches shifted cosine; a numerator 3 matches shifted sine.

Your method checklist

  1. Transform derivatives with their initial-value terms.
  2. Collect Y terms and solve for Y.
  3. Decompose into recognizable transform pairs.
  4. Invert and check the initial values in the final time-domain answer.
