Springs, damping, and circuits

Build a second-order model and connect the solution to physical behaviour.

Before you start: Second-order constant-coefficient equations and basic units.

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Measure displacement from equilibrium

For a spring with mass mmm, damping constant ccc, spring constant kkk, and applied force FFF, mx′′+cx′+kx=F(t).mx''+cx'+kx=F(t).mx′′+cx′+kx=F(t). Here xxx is displacement from the static equilibrium, so gravity is already balanced. Choose a positive direction and use it consistently in the initial velocity and force.

Read damping from the roots

The discriminant c2−4mkc^2-4mkc2−4mk distinguishes overdamped (positive), critically damped (zero), and underdamped (negative) motion. With c=0c=0c=0, the natural angular frequency is ω0=k/m\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}ω0​=k/m​. For underdamping the oscillation frequency is k/m−c2/(4m2)\sqrt{k/m-c^2/(4m^2)}k/m−c2/(4m2)​.

Transient and driven motion

In a forced problem, x=xc+xpx=x_c+x_px=xc​+xp​. With positive damping the complementary part decays. Without damping, sinusoidal forcing at the natural frequency leads to a resonant response with a growing factor ttt. Write Acos⁡ωt+Bsin⁡ωt=Rcos⁡(ωt−ϕ)A\cos\omega t+B\sin\omega t=R\cos(\omega t-\phi)Acosωt+Bsinωt=Rcos(ωt−ϕ), with R=A2+B2R=\sqrt{A^2+B^2}R=A2+B2​ and the correct quadrant for ϕ\phiϕ.

The electrical analogue

For charge qqq in a series LRC circuit, Lq′′+Rq′+1Cq=E(t),i=q′.Lq''+Rq'+\frac{1}{C}q=E(t),\qquad i=q'.Lq′′+Rq′+C1​q=E(t),i=q′. Inductance LLL plays the role of mass, resistance RRR of damping, and 1/C1/C1/C of stiffness. Do not confuse capacitance CCC with an integration constant.

Your method checklist

  1. Define the dependent variable and sign convention.
  2. Write the physical equation with consistent units.
  3. Solve and apply displacement/charge and velocity/current conditions.
  4. Interpret frequency, amplitude, decay, and long-term behaviour.

TRY IT YOURSELF

Your turn

For a circuit with L=1 H, R=0, C=1/4 F, and E=0, find charge and current if q(0)=0 and i(0)=2 A.

Try the method on paper first. The full solution is available whenever you need it.

Show the step-by-step solution
  1. 1

    Translate the parameters

    q′′+4q=0,q(0)=0,q′(0)=2.q''+4q=0,\qquad q(0)=0,\quad q'(0)=2.q′′+4q=0,q(0)=0,q′(0)=2.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Differentiate for current

    Current is the derivative of charge, so i=2cos⁡2ti=2\cos2ti=2cos2t.

RESULTq(t)=sin⁡2t C,i(t)=2cos⁡2t Aq(t)=\sin2t\ \text{C},\qquad i(t)=2\cos2t\ \text{A}q(t)=sin2t C,i(t)=2cos2t A

Check the answer

Avoid this mistake

A frequency in radians per second is not the same as cycles per second: divide by 2π to obtain cycles per second.
