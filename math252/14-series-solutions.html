Solving with a power series

Turn a differential equation into a recurrence that builds the solution one coefficient at a time.

Before you start: Power series and reindexing.

UnderstandMethodExample 1Your turnAvoid this mistake

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

When to try a series

For y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0y''+P(x)y'+Q(x)y=0y′′+P(x)y′+Q(x)y=0, a point is ordinary when PPP and QQQ are analytic there. A power series centered at an ordinary point can represent solutions locally. If the leading coefficient vanishes, normalize and examine the coefficients before assuming an ordinary-point series works.

The recurrence is the engine

Substitute y=∑anxny=\sum a_nx^ny=∑an​xn, write all terms with the same power of xxx, and set each coefficient to zero. For a second-order equation, a0=y(0)a_0=y(0)a0​=y(0) and a1=y′(0)a_1=y'(0)a1​=y′(0) are the two starting values. The recurrence determines the rest.

A finite series is usually an approximation

Keep an ellipsis or a big-O remainder when truncating. A polynomial approximation may satisfy the ODE only to the retained order. Check more than the first two terms to catch index errors.

Your method checklist

  1. Choose the expansion point and check that it is ordinary.
  2. Substitute the series and reindex.
  3. Equate coefficients and solve for the next coefficient.
  4. Use the initial values, then generate several terms.
