Separable equations

Separate the two variables, integrate, and recover the constant solutions that division can hide.

Before you start: Antiderivatives, logarithms, and exponentials.

UnderstandMethodExample 1Your turnAvoid this mistake

THE IDEA

Understand before calculating

Spot the pattern

Use separation when dydx=g(x)h(y).\frac{dy}{dx}=g(x)h(y).dxdy​=g(x)h(y). On a region where h(y)≠0h(y)\ne0h(y)=0, rewrite this as dy/h(y)=g(x) dxdy/h(y)=g(x)\,dxdy/h(y)=g(x)dx. You are using the chain rule in reverse.

Check equilibria first

Any root h(c)=0h(c)=0h(c)=0 gives a constant solution y=cy=cy=c. Dividing by h(y)h(y)h(y) would remove it, so record these solutions before separating.

Handle the constant carefully

Put one arbitrary constant on the final integrated equation. In ln⁡∣y∣=G(x)+C\ln|y|=G(x)+Cln∣y∣=G(x)+C, exponentiation gives y=AeG(x)y=Ae^{G(x)}y=AeG(x) on a nonzero branch. The sign is absorbed into AAA; add the zero solution separately if valid.

Your method checklist

  1. Check for constant solutions.
  2. Collect the y terms with dy and the x terms with dx.
  3. Integrate both sides, then use the initial value.
  4. Solve for y if practical and check the interval.
