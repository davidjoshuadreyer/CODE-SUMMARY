08 · Green’s, divergence, and Stokes’ theorems

Choose the right theorem and keep the boundary orientation consistent.

Green’s theorem converts planar circulation

For a positively oriented, simple closed, piecewise smooth curve bounding a plane region, and a field with continuous first partials on an open set containing the region, ∮C(P dx+Q dy)=∬R(∂Q∂x−∂P∂y)dA.\oint_C(P\,dx+Q\,dy)=\iint_R\left(\frac{\partial Q}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial P}{\partial y}\right)dA.∮C​(Pdx+Qdy)=∬R​(∂x∂Q​−∂y∂P​)dA. Positive orientation is counterclockwise. Clockwise circulation changes the sign. Regions with holes require all boundary components with consistent orientation.

Planar outward flux uses divergence

For the same regularity conditions, the two-dimensional flux form is ∮CF⋅n ds=∬R(∂P∂x+∂Q∂y)dA.\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,ds=\iint_R\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial Q}{\partial y}\right)dA.∮C​F⋅nds=∬R​(∂x∂P​+∂y∂Q​)dA. Here n\mathbf nn is the outward unit normal. Circulation and outward flux are different quantities, even for the same field and curve.

Closed surfaces use the divergence theorem

For a closed, piecewise smooth surface bounding a solid, with outward normal and a continuously differentiable field throughout the solid, ∬∂QF⋅n dS=∭Q∇⋅F dV.\iint_{\partial Q}\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf n\,dS=\iiint_Q\nabla\cdot\mathbf F\,dV.∬∂Q​F⋅ndS=∭Q​∇⋅FdV. An open surface needs its missing cap accounted for. Do not apply the theorem across a point where the field is undefined.

Stokes’ theorem connects a surface to its boundary

For an oriented, piecewise smooth surface and a sufficiently smooth field, ∮∂SF⋅dr=∬S(∇×F)⋅n dS.\oint_{\partial S}\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_S(\nabla\times\mathbf F)\cdot\mathbf n\,dS.∮∂S​F⋅dr=∬S​(∇×F)⋅ndS. The boundary direction follows the right-hand rule relative to the normal. With an upward normal, a horizontal boundary is counterclockwise when viewed from above. WORKED EXAMPLE

Let F=⟨−y,x⟩\mathbf F=\langle-y,x\rangleF=⟨−y,x⟩ around the circle of radius 2, counterclockwise. Green’s theorem gives ∮CF⋅dr=∬R(1−(−1)) dA=2(4π)=8π.\oint_C\mathbf F\cdot d\mathbf r=\iint_R(1-(-1))\,dA=2(4\pi)=8\pi.∮C​F⋅dr=∬R​(1−(−1))dA=2(4π)=8π. In contrast, ∇⋅F=0\nabla\cdot\mathbf F=0∇⋅F=0, so the outward flux across that circle is zero.
