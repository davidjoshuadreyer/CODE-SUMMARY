02 · Tangent planes, gradients, and error

Use derivatives to approximate a surface and measure change in a chosen direction.

Build a tangent plane

At a differentiable point (a,b,f(a,b))(a,b,f(a,b))(a,b,f(a,b)), the tangent plane is z=f(a,b)+fx(a,b)(x−a)+fy(a,b)(y−b).z=f(a,b)+f_x(a,b)(x-a)+f_y(a,b)(y-b).z=f(a,b)+fx​(a,b)(x−a)+fy​(a,b)(y−b). It is also the linear approximation L(x,y)L(x,y)L(x,y). A horizontal tangent plane requires both fx=0f_x=0fx​=0 and fy=0f_y=0fy​=0. For an implicit surface G(x,y,z)=cG(x,y,z)=cG(x,y,z)=c, a nonzero gradient ∇G\nabla G∇G is a normal vector.

Normalize the direction

The gradient ∇f=⟨fx,fy⟩\nabla f=\langle f_x,f_y\rangle∇f=⟨fx​,fy​⟩ points toward fastest local increase when it is nonzero. For direction vector v\mathbf vv, first form a unit vector, then take a dot product: u=v∥v∥,Duf=∇f⋅u.\mathbf u=\frac{\mathbf v}{\|\mathbf v\|},\qquad D_{\mathbf u}f=\nabla f\cdot\mathbf u.u=∥v∥v​,Du​f=∇f⋅u. The maximum directional derivative is ∥∇f∥\|\nabla f\|∥∇f∥, not the sum of its components.

Approximate changes and errors

For small input changes, Δf≈df=fx dx+fy dy\Delta f\approx df=f_x\,dx+f_y\,dyΔf≈df=fx​dx+fy​dy. When f=Cxaybf=Cx^a y^bf=Cxayb is nonzero and the inputs are positive, dff=adxx+bdyy.\frac{df}{f}=a\frac{dx}{x}+b\frac{dy}{y}.fdf​=axdx​+bydy​. A first-order worst-case relative-error estimate adds absolute contributions. It is an approximation; it does not account for higher-order terms.

Find a tangent to an intersection

For two level surfaces G=c1G=c_1G=c1​ and H=c2H=c_2H=c2​, a tangent direction at a regular intersection is ∇G×∇H\nabla G\times\nabla H∇G×∇H. Check that the point lies on both surfaces. If this cross product vanishes, the method does not supply a nonzero tangent direction.
