The little science behind the flight ＋

This is a playful glider model: lift acts across the airflow, drag slows the plane, and weight pulls it down. Both lift and drag grow with airspeed squared. Roll left or right to set your bank angle; it holds when you release the controls. Opposite input levels the wings. Banking reduces upward lift. Pull up to trade speed for height; ease the nose down to recover from a stall. Sun-warmed ground produces localized thermals. Wind forced up a slope produces ridge lift that weakens with height; the sheltered side can sink. Ground effect reduces induced drag close to the surface—it does not add energy. Rain increases drag, while storm cells add gusts. Thrust adds energy from a recharging arcade power reserve.

Wingspan, mass, trim, and upgrades feed the simulation. Distances and forces are tuned for satisfying short flights, rather than predicting a real paper plane. Inspired by NASA’s glider aerodynamics and the FAA’s soaring guidance.
