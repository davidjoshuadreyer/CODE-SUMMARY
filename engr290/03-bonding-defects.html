03 · Bonding and point defects

Explain bond types and calculate equilibrium vacancy changes.

Primary and secondary bonds

Ionic bonding involves electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions; covalent bonding shares electrons and is directional. Metallic bonding has delocalized electrons that help explain electrical conductivity and non-directional bonding. Secondary interactions include dipole attractions and hydrogen bonding. Bonding helps explain properties, but crystal structure and defects also matter.

Classify the defect

A vacancy is an empty regular site. A substitutional atom replaces a host atom; an interstitial occupies a gap. A self-interstitial is a host atom in such a gap. A Frenkel defect pairs a vacancy with an interstitial of the same ion. A Schottky defect removes ions in a charge-neutral stoichiometric combination; in a 1:1 ionic solid this is one cation and one anion vacancy.

Vacancies follow an Arrhenius relation

Write vacancy fraction f = C exp(−Eᵥ/kT), where C is a prefactor. With constant C, f₂/f₁ = exp[(Eᵥ/k)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂)]. Use absolute temperature in kelvin when k is in eV/K, and match the energy units. A plot of ln f against 1/T has slope −Eᵥ/k.

Reason before calculating

If temperature rises, equilibrium vacancy fraction rises. If your result falls, check the sign of the exponent. When an initial fraction is supplied, use the ratio form; setting C = 1 unnecessarily can discard the given calibration.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Using the assignment values, T₁ = 673.15 K, T₂ = 933.15 K, Eᵥ = 0.76 eV, k = 8.62 × 10⁻⁵ eV/K and f₁ = 2.29 × 10⁻⁵: f₂ = f₁ exp[(0.76/k)(1/673.15 − 1/933.15)] ≈ 8.80 × 10⁻⁴. This is a fraction, or about 0.0880%.

Check your understanding

Why does a vacancy–interstitial pair preserve charge neutrality when an ion moves from its lattice site to an interstitial site?

Show the worked answer

The ion stays in the solid. Relative to the perfect lattice, its missing-site charge and extra-interstitial charge cancel; this is a Frenkel defect.
